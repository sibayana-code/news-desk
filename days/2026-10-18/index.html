Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Matthew 22:15-21

The Pharisees went off and plotted how they might entrap Jesus in speech. They sent their disciples to him, with the Herodians, saying, “Teacher, we know that you are a truthful man and that you teach the way of God in accordance with the truth. And you are not concerned with anyone’s opinion, for you do not regard a person’s status. Tell us, then, what is your opinion: Is it lawful to pay the census tax to Caesar or not?” Knowing their malice, Jesus said, “Why are you testing me, you hypocrites? Show me the coin that pays the census tax.” Then they handed him the Roman coin. He said to them, “Whose image is this and whose inscription?” They replied, “Caesar’s.” At that he said to them, “Then repay to Caesar what belongs to Caesar and to God what belongs to God.”

The words of the Popes

Jesus replies with a surprising political realism, linked to the theocentrism of the prophetic tradition. The tribute to Caesar must be paid because his image is on the coin; but the human being, every person, carries in him- or herself another image, that of God, and therefore it is to him and to him alone that each one owes his or her existence. The Fathers of the Church, drawing inspiration from the fact that Jesus was referring to the image of the Emperor impressed on the coin of the tribute, interpreted this passage in the light of the fundamental concept of the human being as an image of God, contained in the first chapter of the Book of Genesis. (…)

This word of Jesus is rich in anthropological content and it cannot be reduced only to the political context. The Church, therefore, is not limited to reminding human beings of the right distinction between the sphere of Caesar’s authority and that of God, between the political and religious contexts. The mission of the Church, like that of Christ, is essentially to speak of God, to remember his sovereignty, to remind all, especially Christians who have lost their own identity, of the right of God to what belongs to him, that is, our life. (Pope Benedict XVI, Homily, 16 October 2011)

Word

render /ˈrɛndə(r)/ — (verb) to give or pay what is due; to hand over, deliver, or provide something owed or requested.

From Middle English rendren, via Old French rendre “to give back,” from Vulgar Latin *rendere, an alteration of Latin reddere (re- “back” + dare “to give”).

render — Matthew: repay to Caesar what is Caesar’s and to God what is God’s. Isaiah: the Lord calls even Cyrus by name. Benedict XVI: the coin bears Caesar’s image, but every person bears God’s, so our life is what we render to him. The Way 353: no leaving the faith at the door like a hat.

Takes

  • ₱89 is the new floor for a branded rice bowl, and it comes from inside the Jollibee group, under Jollibee’s own ₱99. If your value line sits at ₱110–₱130 with no clear protein or topping story, you’re now priced against a drive-thru. Don’t match it. Build one bowl at ₱99–₱109 that looks bigger (egg, crunchy topping, visible sauce) and earn the margin on the drink. Open
  • Coffee is splitting in two: Compose at ₱90 for a 20-oz Americano, Bacha at ₱399 with theatre and gift boxes. A ₱150–₱220 café cup with neither price nor show is the one in trouble. Mang Inasal goes to ₱89 with Sulit Bowls, ten pesos under Jollibee’s own value line

17 Oct 2026

Wazzup.PH: Mang Inasal launched Sulit Bowls from ₱89 in Metro Manila and Luzon, with Visayas and Mindanao due within the month. There are two variants. Soy Garlic Chicken is grilled chicken with garlic bits, spring onion, sliced egg and soy-garlic sauce over rice, and Pork Sisig is sisig with creamy sauce over rice. They sell through dine-in, takeout, drive-thru and delivery. President Mike V. Castro pitched it as delicious, filling, convenient and affordable. It lands while sister brand Jollibee runs Burger Steak bundles from ₱99 through Dec 31.

Operator read: both bowls are built on cost-friendly protein. Sisig runs on pork, and PSA had fresh pork with bones at ₱295.58/kg in late September, down from ₱327.03 a year earlier. Chicken in a bowl stretches less meat across more rice. A single-vessel bowl is also faster on the line and travels better on delivery than a plated meal. For an independent the copy-paste move is format, not price: one or two rice bowls built on a cheaper cut, an egg and a signature sauce, priced just above ₱100, with a drink attach. Watch whether JFC’s brands end up competing with each other on price, because a ₱89–₱99 band held through Christmas resets what guests expect a quick lunch to cost. Flag: brand release, no volume or margin data.

Bacha Coffee bets ₱399 a cup on Manila’s 8 pm coffee queues

17 Oct 2026

ABS-CBN: Bacha Coffee, founded in Marrakech in 1910 and now in 16 countries with more than 50 branches, opened at SM Mall of Asia and Greenbelt 3 this month. Owner and chief commercial officer Maranda Barnes said the Philippines stood out for its coffee culture, with people queuing for coffee even at 8 pm. A cup is ₱399 and comes with vanilla Chantilly cream. The stores retail 200-plus 100% arabica varieties from 35 countries, ground to order and packed in canisters or gift boxes, and serve croissants, breakfast and lunch. Barnes pointed Filipino diners to a soy-marinated chicken croissant she likened to adobo. The goal she named is becoming a household name.

Operator read: this is coffee sold as a gift and an occasion, not a caffeine hit. Two months after Jollibee brought Compose Coffee in at ₱90, the market now has both ends served by well-funded brands. The playbook worth stealing isn’t the price. It’s the retail wall: beans, canisters and gift boxes turn a café into a Christmas shop with no extra seats. If you run coffee in a Calamba or Nuvali site, a small seasonal gifting shelf is a low-risk way to lift the ticket in November and December. Flag: brand interview at launch, no sales data.
