Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 17:26-37

Jesus said to his disciples: “As it was in the days of Noah, so it will be in the days of the Son of Man; they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage up to the day that Noah entered the ark, and the flood came and destroyed them all. Similarly, as it was in the days of Lot: they were eating, drinking, buying, selling, planting, building; on the day when Lot left Sodom, fire and brimstone rained from the sky to destroy them all. So it will be on the day the Son of Man is revealed. On that day, someone who is on the housetop and whose belongings are in the house must not go down to get them, and likewise one in the field must not return to what was left behind. Remember the wife of Lot. Whoever seeks to preserve his life will lose it, but whoever loses it will save it. I tell you, on that night there will be two people in one bed; one will be taken, the other left. And there will be two women grinding meal together; one will be taken, the other left.” They said to him in reply, “Where, Lord?” He said to them, “Where the body is, there also the vultures will gather.”

The words of the Popes

How will the Lord come ?(…)Jesus says that it will take place as in “the days of Noah”(…).And what did they do in the days of Noah? Just the normal, everyday things of life, as always: “eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage”(…).Let us bear this in mind: God is hidden in our life, he is always there — he is concealed in the most common and most ordinary situations in our life. He does not come in extraordinary events, but in everyday things. He manifests himself in everyday things. He is there, in our daily work, in a chance encounter, in the face of someone in need, even when we face days that seem grey and monotonous. It is right there that we find the Lord, who calls to us, speaks to us and inspires our actions. However, there is a second question: how can we recognize and welcome the Lord? We must be awake, alert, vigilant. Jesus warns us: there is the danger we may not notice his coming and may be unprepared for his visit.(…) Indeed, Jesus says that those people in the time of Noah ate and drank “and they did not know until the flood came and swept them all away”(…) Therefore, brothers and sisters, let us remain vigilant! Waiting for the Lord to come, waiting for the Lord to draw near to us, because he is there, but waiting alert. (Pope Francis, Angelus, 27 November 2022)

Word

vigilant /ˈvɪdʒɪlənt/ (adjective) — keeping careful watch for possible danger or difficulty; alert and wakeful.

Shakey's Pizza posts Q3 2026 earnings rebound as reset strategy pays off

12 Nov 2026 · Context.ph

Shakey's Pizza Asia Ventures (SPAVI) says core net income rose 11% year on year in Q3 and 51% from Q2, excluding one-time restructuring costs, which came mostly from scaling back its Peri-Peri Charcoal Chicken network. Systemwide sales reached ₱6.8B (up 11%), and same-store sales grew 3.5% after a flat first half. Management credits value promotions and price increases kept below inflation. Gross margin improved 100 basis points from Q2 to 21.2%, which it attributes to supply chain savings. The group has 3,106 stores and outlets, with 136 net new this year. Including the restructuring charges, headline net income was only ₱75M for the quarter. CEO Vicente Gregorio calls 2026 a "reset year".

Operator read: this is one public company's numbers showing that holding prices and running promos brought customers back. Margin came back through purchasing and supply chain savings, not through menu prices. Its weak spot was a secondary brand that grew too many stores and had to be cut back. If you run a second concept, put it through the same test: does each store pay for itself? With rice and pork prices unsettled into the holidays, the lesson is to hold the line on price and win the savings back in your supply chain.

DA says pork prices down despite farmers' complaints

12 Nov 2026 · Daily Tribune

Malacañang, citing DA monitoring, says retail pork prices are down from February. Local kasim went from ₱350 to ₱320/kg and local belly from ₱385 to ₱370/kg, while imported belly stayed at ₱300. Farm group SINAG counters that wet-market pork is still overpriced when farmgate prices are only ₱180–200/kg. The Federation of Free Farmers welcomed the government's 60-day suspension of some pork imports but says it came too late: imports and frozen stocks had already pushed farmgate prices below what it costs hog raisers to produce.

Operator read: the gap between what farms get and what markets charge is your opening. Hog raisers are losing money and want buyers. A restaurant group in Laguna or Batangas can sign directly with a raiser or a cooperative and an accredited slaughterhouse for steady weekly volume at a price tied to farmgate. The import pause could tighten frozen supply in December, so stock up on imported belly cuts now if your menu depends on them.
