Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 21:1-4

When Jesus looked up he saw some wealthy people putting their offerings into the treasury and he noticed a poor widow putting in two small coins. He said, “I tell you truly, this poor widow put in more than all the rest; for those others have all made offerings from their surplus wealth, but she, from her poverty, has offered her whole livelihood.”

The words of the Popes

Jesus invites us to watch the poor widow. The Lord denounces the exploitation of this woman, who, in making her offering, must return home without even the little she had to live on. How important it is to free the sacred from ties with money! Jesus had already said it elsewhere: you cannot serve two masters. Either you serve God — and we think he will say “or the devil”, no — either God or money. He is a master, and Jesus says we must not serve him. But, at the same time, Jesus praises the fact that this widow puts all she has into the treasury. She has nothing left, but finds her everything in God. She is not afraid of losing the little she has because she trusts in God’s abundance, and God’s abundance multiplies the joy of those who give. (…) Lord always goes further, is more generous. But it is He, not our avarice. This is why Jesus proposes her as a teacher of faith, this woman: she does not go to the Temple to clear her conscience, she does not pray to make herself seen, she does not show off her faith, but she gives from her heart generously and freely. The sound of her few coins is more beautiful than the grandiose offerings of the rich, since they express a life sincerely dedicated to God, a faith that does not live by appearances but by unconditional trust. Let us learn from her: a faith without external frills, but interiorly sincere; a faith composed of humble love for God and for our brothers and sisters. (Pope Francis, Angelus, 7 November 2021)

Word

mite /maɪt/ (noun) — a very small coin or sum of money; a small but heartfelt contribution, as in “the widow’s mite.”

From Middle Dutch mīte, a small copper coin; the phrase “widow’s mite” comes from the King James rendering of Mark 12:42.

mite — Luke 21: the poor widow’s two small coins outweigh the offerings of the rich, because she gave “her whole livelihood.” Pope Francis: “The sound of her few coins is more beautiful than the grandiose offerings of the rich.” The Way 466: give, and don’t let the left hand know what the right is doing.

Takes

  • When the head of the restaurant owners' association says fuel is taking your customers' money, the December check is at risk, not just January's. Push value you can see (a clear solo set, a fixed-price bundle) rather than blanket discounts, which cut margin you need for Q1 costs.
  • Modern Shanghai's ₱388 solo sets are the same answer from a group operator: a family-style menu adds a solo price point so the off-peak weekday diner has a reason to come in. Look at your own menu for big-plate items you can break down into a solo plate.
  • Nuvali gets two big malls' worth of food competition in the same month. If you're scouting Santa Rosa, wait for 60–90 days of foot traffic data after the 27 Nov opening before signing. Rents ask high at launch, and the early weeks run on curiosity.

RestoPH: restaurants are now competing with gas stations for Filipinos' budget

22 Nov 2026 · Bilyonaryo News Channel

David Sison, president of the Restaurant Owners of the Philippines Association (RestoPH), told BNC's "Follow the Money" that pressure on the industry started in February, when the Iran war pushed fuel prices up. Diners have become more careful with money and want better value. That forces operators, especially small ones, to rethink what they offer. His line: during Covid you had to pivot to delivery, and now you have to pivot to real value. He called the sector very competitive because it is easy to enter, which makes it hard to stay in business.

Operator read: fuel works on you twice. It raises your own costs (deliveries, staff transport, LPG-adjacent prices), and it leaves the customer less to spend. Value doesn't have to mean cheaper. It can be a protected solo set, an honest portion, or a bundle with a price people remember, while the top of the menu keeps its margin. For an opener, the easy entry Sison describes cuts both ways. Weak concepts will close through Q1, so second-generation restaurant spaces with kitchens already fitted will come up at better terms. Be ready with cash rather than a fit-out budget.

Chagee opens a destination flagship at Bonifacio High Street

22 Nov 2026 · The Manila Times

Chinese tea chain Chagee opened its first Philippine flagship at Bonifacio High Street, Taguig, on 13 Nov. The design borrows from the bahay kubo: wood and stone, solihiya-style screens, local woven textiles, and a large mural by Filipino artist Raxenne Maniquiz. It also launched a BO·YA Mango Jasmine Green series for the local market. GM Christopher "Sonny" Tiong said the plan from the start was to open the market with a flagship, and that the brand wanted a "cultural destination", not just another tea shop.

Operator read: in the same week RestoPH talks value, a foreign drinks brand spends on a showcase store with seating and a local look. That's the split in the market: the middle gets squeezed while destination formats and value formats both find customers. The local-flavor SKU and local design are cheap ways to make a foreign brand feel at home. Filipino concepts can do the same in reverse by making local design the reason to visit. If you sell drinks or desserts near BGC, expect Chagee's flagship to set the bar for dwell time in the area.
