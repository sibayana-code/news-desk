Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Galatians 2:1-2, 7-14

Brothers and sisters: After fourteen years I again went up to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus along also. I went up in accord with a revelation, and I presented to them the Gospel that I preach to the Gentiles, but privately to those of repute, so that I might not be running, or have run, in vain.

On the contrary, when they saw that I had been entrusted with the Gospel to the uncircumcised, just as Peter to the circumcised, for the one who worked in Peter for an apostolate to the circumcised worked also in me for the Gentiles, and when they recognized the grace bestowed upon me, James and Cephas and John, who were reputed to be pillars, gave me and Barnabas their right hands in partnership, that we should go to the Gentiles and they to the circumcised. Only, we were to be mindful of the poor, which is the very thing I was eager to do.

And when Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face because he clearly was wrong. For, until some people came from James, he used to eat with the Gentiles; but when they came, he began to draw back and separated himself, because he was afraid of the circumcised. And the rest of the Jews acted hypocritically along with him, with the result that even Barnabas was carried away by their hypocrisy. But when I saw that they were not on the right road in line with the truth of the Gospel, I said to Cephas in front of all, “If you, though a Jew, are living like a Gentile and not like a Jew, how can you compel the Gentiles to live like Jews?”

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 11:1-4

Jesus was praying in a certain place, and when he had finished, one of his disciples said to him, “Lord, teach us to pray just as John taught his disciples.” He said to them, “When you pray, say: Father, hallowed be your name, your Kingdom come. Give us each day our daily bread and forgive us our sins for we ourselves forgive everyone in debt to us, and do not subject us to the final test.”

The words of the Popes

“Do not be afraid”. This is the essential element of a vocation: for man is afraid. (…) Do not be afraid, for you have found Grace; do not be afraid of life, do not be afraid of your motherhood, do not be afraid of your marriage, do not be afraid of your priesthood, for you have found Grace. This certainty, this awareness, helps us just as it helped Mary. (…) And so, all people are born through your “yes”. You must know this: such a “yes”, in imitation of Mary, such a “yes” creates joy, a new life, a breath, a blessing. (…) And so, by analogy, in due proportion, your ‘yes’, your fidelity – whether as husband, wife, young person, doctor or professor – your own distinct “yes” creates joy; the world is reborn; and human life – in its various dimensions, in the social sphere, in different settings such as the family, the parish and the nation – becomes more human, thanks to this “yes”. (Saint John Paul II, Address to the Pontifical Roman Major Seminary, 25 March 1982)

7.2% inflation: the market went up 6.8%, the restaurant menu only 3.4%

6 Oct 2026

PSA, BusinessWorld and DA: headline inflation hit 7.2% in September, up from 6.1% in August and 1.7% a year ago. That matches April and is the fastest since March 2023, though still inside BSP’s 6.4–7.4% range. Food rose 6.8%, from 4.6%. Rice rose 20.3%, its highest in over two years, and regular-milled averaged ₱49.97/kg in the second half of September, against ₱40.11 a year earlier. Vegetables swung from a 3.4% decline to a 10.7% rise after the floods. Fish rose 7.4% and fruit 8.7%. Per DA, cereals alone made up 64.5% of food inflation. BSP added that meat inflation picked up on renewed swine-flu concerns. Outside food, LPG rose 30%, electricity 14.6% and the utilities group 8.4%. PSA’s Claire Dennis Mapa warned the Sept 28 public-transport fare hikes aren’t counted yet, and DA expects the worst of the dry spell from November to April. Operator read: the useful number is the gap. Ready-made food, which is restaurant and takeout pricing, rose only 3.4% while raw food rose 6.8%. The trade has been eating the cost, and that can’t last through an El Niño summer. Move menu prices in October while customers are reading “7.2%” in the headlines and a price change looks normal. Push rice-heavy, vegetable and fish items first, and use smaller servings or bundles where a peso increase would hurt traffic. Don’t lean on pork promos anymore: with swine-flu worries back, lock a 60–90 day pork price now. LPG at +30% makes gas-heavy menus (fryers, woks, long braises) the place to check yield and holding times. Your staff also feel the fare hike from next month, so plan for that in schedules and transport allowances.

Paris Baguette’s 8th PH store sells galbi and kimchi fried rice to win lunch

6 Oct 2026

Herald Business (Seoul): Paris Baguette has opened its eighth Philippine store at Glorieta in Makati. The brand entered in April 2024 at Mall of Asia and added a NAIA Terminal 3 store last year. The menu leads with Korean-inspired meals, including grilled chicken galbi, minced pork galbi and Spam kimchi fried rice, alongside bakery items made with local ingredients and a Philippines-only ube Parmesan Basque cheesecake. Operator read: eight stores in two and a half years is a slow build. The real news is the menu. A bakery that sells rice meals is going after lunch and merienda customers in the same mall, not just coffee-and-pastry ones. That is the Korean playbook (Compose Coffee franchising, Paris Baguette meals) closing in on the Filipino rice-meal-plus-dessert counter. For a local concept, the defense is what imports price poorly: a proper ulam-and-rice plate under ₱200 and a local dessert hook, with ube already a crowd-pleaser. The open question for south Metro is whether SM Nuvali’s Nov 27 opening gets one.
