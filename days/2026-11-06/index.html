Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Philippians
3:17—4:1

Join with others in being imitators of me, brothers and sisters, 
and observe those who thus conduct themselves 
according to the model you have in us.
For many, as I have often told you 
and now tell you even in tears, 
conduct themselves as enemies of the cross of Christ.
Their end is destruction.
Their God is their stomach; 
their glory is in their “shame.”
Their minds are occupied with earthly things.
But our citizenship is in heaven, 
and from it we also await a savior, the Lord Jesus Christ.
He will change our lowly body
to conform with his glorified Body 
by the power that enables him also 
to bring all things into subjection to himself.

Therefore, my brothers and sisters,
whom I love and long for, my joy and crown, 
in this way stand firm in the Lord, beloved.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke
16:1-8

Jesus said to his disciples, “A rich man had a steward
who was reported to him for squandering his property.
He summoned him and said,
‘What is this I hear about you?
Prepare a full account of your stewardship,
because you can no longer be my steward.’
The steward said to himself, ‘What shall I do,
now that my master is taking the position of steward away from me?
I am not strong enough to dig and I am ashamed to beg.
I know what I shall do so that,
when I am removed from the stewardship,
they may welcome me into their homes.’
He called in his master’s debtors one by one.
To the first he said, ‘How much do you owe my master?’
He replied, ‘One hundred measures of olive oil.’
He said to him, ‘Here is your promissory note.
Sit down and quickly write one for fifty.’
Then to another he said, ‘And you, how much do you owe?’
He replied, ‘One hundred measures of wheat.’ 
He said to him, ‘Here is your promissory note;
write one for eighty.’
And the master commended that dishonest steward for acting prudently.
For the children of this world
are more prudent in dealing with their own generation
than the children of light.”

The words of the Popes

In the story we see a steward summoned by the rich man for “an accounting” of his management. Here we are presented with an image that conveys something important: we are not the masters of our lives or of the goods we enjoy; everything has been given to us as a gift by the Lord, who has entrusted this to our care, our freedom, and our responsibility. One day we will be called to give an account of how we have managed ourselves, our possessions and the earth’s resources – before both God and humankind, before society, and especially before those who will come after us. (…)

Taking his cue from the story, Jesus exhorts us: “Make friends for yourselves by means of dishonest wealth so that when it is gone, they may welcome you into the eternal homes” (v. 9).

Indeed, the steward in the parable, even while managing the dishonest wealth of this world, succeeds in finding a way to make friends, leaving behind the solitude of his own selfishness. How much more, then, must we, who are disciples living in the light of the Gospel, use the goods of this world and our very lives with a view to the true wealth, which is friendship with the Lord and with our brothers and sisters. (Pope Leo XIV, Angelus, 21 September 2025)

steward /ˈstuːərd/ (noun) — a person who manages the property or affairs of another; historically, a chief administrator of a household or manor.

From Middle English steward, from Old English stiġweard “house-guardian,” from stiġ “hall, enclosure” + weard “ward, guard.”

DA seeks to sustain easing food inflation despite cost pressures

5 Nov 2026 · Department of Agriculture

Food inflation for the bottom 30% of households slowed to 9.4% in October from 9.8% in September, according to PSA figures the DA cited. Fish and seafood (6.4% from 8.4%) and vegetables, tubers and pulses (7.9% from 9%) did most of the cooling, and oils and fats eased slightly. Corn sped up to 21.5%, fruit to 10.8%, and bread, flour and bakery products also rose faster. Rice stayed at 23.6%, since prices a year ago were falling, while meat dropped 3.6%. Cereals made up 78% of food inflation for poorer households. Secretary Tiu Laurel said the DA will keep fuel and toll subsidies, lower feed-corn tariffs and expand the Benteng Bigas rice program.

Operator read: the cost picture has split. Proteins and produce are giving you room, while starches and baked goods keep eating margin, and rice is the one every Filipino plate carries. If your menu leans on unli-rice, bread service or pastries, those lines are where the squeeze is. Rebalance portions toward the plate items that are getting cheaper rather than raising headline prices. Your customers are feeling the same rice and bread inflation, so value meals built around fish, chicken and vegetables are the easier sell this quarter. The feed-corn tariff cut is worth watching too, because cheaper feed eventually shows up as cheaper chicken and pork.

Taste of Joy disputes customer's viral claim of fly droppings in palabok

5 Nov 2026 · Radar

A customer posted on a Muntinlupa community Facebook page that they found what looked like fly droppings in a palabok order from Taste of Joy's Bayanan branch; the post has since been deleted. The business says the two medium bilaos were delivered between 11:30 AM and noon on 1 Nov and that it first heard of the complaint at about 7:40 PM. The second bilao was opened more than seven hours after delivery, it says, while its packaging tells customers to eat right away or refrigerate within two hours, and riders repeat that. It offered a replacement that evening and said refunds need management approval. It also questioned how the bilao in the customer's video was tied.

Operator read: this is the risk in every tray, bilao and party order you send out. Once food leaves your hands you can't prove its condition, so your defense has to be built in before it goes out: a printed packed-at time, a clear consume-or-refrigerate window, a tamper seal, and a delivery time on the receipt. Taste of Joy had most of that, which is why the community argument went both ways. The weak spot was the refund line. Saying refunds need management approval reads as stalling online, so give your branch managers a fixed authority to replace or refund on the spot, and settle complaints before they become posts.
