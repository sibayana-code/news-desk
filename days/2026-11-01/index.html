Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Matthew 5:1-12a

When Jesus saw the crowds, he went up the mountain, and after he had sat down, his disciples came to him. He began to teach them, saying:

"Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the Kingdom of heaven. Blessed are they who mourn, for they will be comforted. Blessed are the meek, for they will inherit the land. Blessed are they who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be satisfied. Blessed are the merciful, for they will be shown mercy. Blessed are the clean of heart, for they will see God. Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called children of God. Blessed are they who are persecuted for the sake of righteousness, for theirs is the Kingdom of heaven. Blessed are you when they insult you and persecute you and utter every kind of evil against you falsely because of me. Rejoice and be glad, for your reward will be great in heaven."

The words of the Popes

The Beatitudes bring with them a new interpretation of reality. They are both the path and the message of Jesus the Teacher. At first glance, it seems impossible to declare as blessed those who are poor, or those who hunger and thirst for justice, the persecuted or the peacemakers. Yet, that which seems inconceivable in the world’s thinking is filled with meaning and light when brought into contact with the Kingdom of God. In the saints, we see this kingdom approaching and becoming present among us. Saint Matthew rightly presents the Beatitudes as a teaching, depicting Jesus as a Master, who transmits a new perspective on things, which is reflected in his own journey. The Beatitudes, however, are not just another teaching; they are the teaching par excellence. In the same way, the Lord Jesus is not just one of many teachers, he is the Master par excellence. Moreover, he is the Educator par excellence. We are his disciples and are in his “school.” We learn how to discover in his life, namely in the path he has travelled, a horizon of meaning capable of shining a light on all forms of knowledge. (Pope Leo XIV, Homily, 1 November 2025)

Word

beatitude /biˈætɪˌtjuːd/ — (noun) supreme blessedness or happiness; in Christian use, each of the blessings Jesus declares in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5:3–12).

From Middle French béatitude, from Latin beatitudo “happiness, blessedness,” from beatus “blessed, happy,” past participle of beare “to bless.”

DA: Nearly 1M Filipinos benefited from ₱20/kg rice in September

31 Oct 2026

The Department of Agriculture says 961,346 people bought rice under the ₱20/kg program in September, the highest monthly turnout so far, buying 21.4 million kilos of locally produced rice. The previous record was 838,695 in April. From January to September the program reached more than 5.6 million Filipinos and sold 135.98 million kilos. Secretary Tiu Laurel has ordered that, starting in December, vulnerable beneficiaries can claim their whole 30-kilo monthly allocation in one transaction, which also frees up NFA warehouse space. The program runs until June 2028. DA price monitoring puts Metro Manila retail rice at ₱45 to ₱170 per kilo depending on type.

Operator read: this is a government-run discount rice channel that is now growing, not winding down. It won’t move your own rice cost, since restaurants buy commercial grades and imported rice is still expected to rise in November. But it shifts what budget customers spend money on. Families who get 30 kilos at ₱20 have more left over for viands, merienda and treats. For a carinderia-style or value QSR menu in Laguna towns, push solid ulam at a clear price point, and don’t count on rice as the draw. Watch whether the December one-trip claim pulls foot traffic toward NFA outlets and the stores next to them.

Open GMA News

Korean and Japanese chains keep opening in Manila: bhc at NAIA, NeNe Chicken shared stores, Katsuya

27–28 Oct 2026

Korean chicken brand bhc, run in the Philippines by Suyen Corp. under a master franchise signed last year, opened its second store in the premium food zone on the fourth floor of NAIA Terminal 3. It is about 162 sqm with 68 seats, and it runs a streamlined menu built around one- and two-piece chicken combos because Filipino customers prefer to buy chicken by the piece. NeNe Chicken opened its second and third Philippine outlets after Makati, and both share premises with Korean brand Bongousse Rice Burger. Japan’s Arclands opened the first Philippine Katsuya tonkatsu store in Metro Manila in late September, looking abroad as its home market struggles, and Torikizoku entered BGC in October through Davao’s JTC Group.

Operator read: these brands bring their menus and supply from abroad but rely on local partners for sites and crews, so competition for good mall spaces and experienced kitchen staff keeps rising in Metro Manila first. Two details are worth copying. bhc cut its menu for a high-rent transit store and sells by the piece, which means fewer items, faster tickets and a lower price per order. NeNe and Bongousse split one lease between two brands. If you’re testing a second concept, putting it inside an existing store before signing a new lease is the cheaper bet. Expect these brands to move south once Metro Manila proves out, so watch Nuvali and the Sta. Rosa malls for leasing signs.
