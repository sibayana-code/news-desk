Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Ephesians 1:15-23

Brothers and sisters: Hearing of your faith in the Lord Jesus and of your love for all the holy ones, I do not cease giving thanks for you, remembering you in my prayers, that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you a spirit of wisdom and revelation resulting in knowledge of him. May the eyes of your hearts be enlightened, that you may know what is the hope that belongs to his call, what are the riches of glory in his inheritance among the holy ones, and what is the surpassing greatness of his power for us who believe, in accord with the exercise of his great might, which he worked in Christ, raising him from the dead and seating him at his right hand in the heavens, far above every principality, authority, power, and dominion, and every name that is named not only in this age but also in the one to come. And he put all things beneath his feet and gave him as head over all things to the Church, which is his Body, the fullness of the one who fills all things in every way.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 12:8-12

Jesus said to his disciples: “I tell you, everyone who acknowledges me before others the Son of Man will acknowledge before the angels of God. But whoever denies me before others will be denied before the angels of God.

“Everyone who speaks a word against the Son of Man will be forgiven, but the one who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven. When they take you before synagogues and before rulers and authorities, do not worry about how or what your defense will be or about what you are to say. For the Holy Spirit will teach you at that moment what you should say.”

The words of the Popes

The Consoler, the Holy Spirit, will be with the Apostles; when Christ will no longer be on earth, the Spirit will be present during the long periods of affliction that will last for centuries (cf. Jn 16:17). The Spirit will therefore be with the Church and within the Church, especially in times of struggle and persecution, as Jesus Himself promises the Apostles in the words recorded in the Synoptic Gospels: “And when they bring you before the synagogues and the rulers and the authorities, do not be anxious how or what you are to answer or what you are to say; for the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say” (Lk 12:11-13; cf. Mk 13:11): “for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father speaking through you” (Mt 10). These words refer to the tribulations endured by the Apostles and by the Christians of the communities they founded and led; but they also refer to all those who, wherever they may be on earth, throughout the ages, will have to suffer for Christ. And in truth, there are many who, throughout the ages, including in recent times, have experienced this help from the Holy Spirit. And they know, and can bear witness, to the joy of the spiritual victory which the Holy Spirit has granted them to achieve. The entire Church today knows this, and bears witness to it. (Saint John Paul II, General Audience, 13 March 1991)

Word

acknowledge /əkˈnɒlɪdʒ/ — (verb) to admit or accept that something is true; to recognize someone openly, or to show that you have noticed them.

From Middle English aknowlechen, a blend of aknowen (Old English oncnāwan, “to understand, recognize”) and knowlechen, “to admit, confess.”

acknowledge — Luke: whoever acknowledges Christ before others will be acknowledged before the angels, and the Spirit will give the words when you are hauled before rulers. Ephesians: may the eyes of your hearts be enlightened. John Paul II: the Spirit stays with the Church in times of struggle. Ignatius of Antioch, whose memorial it is, acknowledged Christ all the way to the arena. The Way 33: we dodge the heart of the matter, always through fear.

Takes

  • Don’t read ₱49.79 as the rice problem fading. The fertilizer shock is a lagged cost: PIDS research has rice up ₱5.0–₱10.6/kg over two to three years from the fertilizer channel alone, and IFAD is now saying the harvest effect is showing up. If your rice supplier will hold price for 60–90 days at today’s level, take it this month. Open
  • Unlimited rice is the line that breaks first. If you run it, or a rice-heavy value meal, price it on a ₱55–₱60/kg landed assumption rather than today’s ₱50 and see if it still clears margin. If it doesn’t, change the rules now (one refill, or unlimited only on dine-in combos) while it still looks like a menu refresh and not a hike.
  • The Lunch Lady is the playbook for a split daypart: cheap, fast noodle soup at lunch and a higher-check charcoal and cocktail dinner, in one lease and one kitchen. IFAD: the Hormuz shock is reaching harvests, and the Philippines buys 90% of its fertilizer

15 Oct 2026

Reuters, via Arab News: International Fund for Agricultural Development president Alvaro Lario said Thursday that the impact of the Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption is “now starting to be seen” in agricultural harvests. Fertilizer and fuel stocks cushioned the first months. Now prolonged uncertainty means more cautious planting, less fertilizer applied, and higher fuel costs for mechanized harvesting. He added El Niño and Black Sea wheat disruptions to the cost stack, and warned that small farmers may shift from higher-value, slower crops to faster-cash ones. A separate official told Reuters that if the US and Iran fail to agree within weeks on a UN-backed plan for moving fertilizer through Hormuz, parts of Asia and Africa could face a food security crisis.

The local numbers line up. PIDS research says the Philippines imports about 90% of its fertilizer and modeled regular-milled rice rising 9.1% to 19.4%, roughly ₱5.0 to ₱10.6/kg, from the fertilizer channel alone, with full adjustment over two to three years. UNDP put domestic urea up about 37% after the shock, from around ₱1,650 to ₱2,255 per 50-kg bag. Meanwhile PSA’s first October reading has rice at ₱49.79/kg, slightly below late September, and DA’s September data has rice inflation at 20.3%.

Operator read: the shelf price is flat-ish while the farm cost is still climbing. That gap usually closes against the buyer. Three moves: (1) ask your rice and corn-fed protein suppliers for a held price through year-end and take a modest premium for it; (2) re-cost any unlimited-rice or rice-heavy value meal at a higher landed price before the Christmas menu locks; (3) watch the shift to faster-cash crops, because it can thin out the slower vegetables and fruit you take for granted. Flag: foreign wire story plus research projections, not a DA price announcement.

The Lunch Lady opens Oct 29 in BGC: noodle soup at lunch, charcoal and cocktails at dinner

15 Oct 2026

Insider PH: The Lunch Lady, which started in 1995 as Nguyễn Thị Thành’s rotating noodle-soup stall in Ho Chi Minh City and blew up after Anthony Bourdain featured it in 2009, opens its Manila restaurant on Oct 29 at 102 Forbestown Road, BGC. It’s the group’s return to Southeast Asia after Vancouver and Toronto, where it has collected Michelin Bib Gourmands. The local partner is HTCG Premium Food Concepts, led by Annabelle Chua. The menu runs two programs: traditional Vietnamese noodle soups at lunch and contemporary Southeast Asian cooking at dinner, with Wagyu phở, steak lúc lắc and garlic noodles, charcoal cooking, local sourcing and craft cocktails. The same week, Reese Fernandez’s Tara Filipino Diner opened its own BGC branch.

Operator read: BGC keeps pulling in foreign brands with a story and a local operator partner, and Forbestown is getting crowded. For an opener outside the capital, the lesson is the format, not the zip code. One kitchen running a fast, cheap lunch and a slower, higher-check dinner gets more out of rent than an all-day menu that’s mediocre at both. Watch the first month’s lunch price point and queue. If phở at lunch lands near QSR value-meal money while dinner carries the margin, that’s a template that travels to a Nuvali or Calamba office-plus-family catchment. Flag: launch announcement, so no pricing or traffic data yet.
