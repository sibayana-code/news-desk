Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Ephesians 1:11-14

Brothers and sisters: In Christ we were also chosen, destined in accord with the purpose of the One who accomplishes all things according to the intention of his will, so that we might exist for the praise of his glory, we who first hoped in Christ. In him you also, who have heard the word of truth, the Gospel of your salvation, and have believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, which is the first installment of our inheritance toward redemption as God’s possession, to the praise of his glory.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 12:1-7

At that time: So many people were crowding together that they were trampling one another underfoot. Jesus began to speak, first to his disciples, “Beware of the leaven–that is, the hypocrisy–of the Pharisees.

“There is nothing concealed that will not be revealed, nor secret that will not be known. Therefore whatever you have said in the darkness will be heard in the light, and what you have whispered behind closed doors will be proclaimed on the housetops. I tell you, my friends, do not be afraid of those who kill the body but after that can do no more. I shall show you whom to fear. Be afraid of the one who after killing has the power to cast into Gehenna; yes, I tell you, be afraid of that one. Are not five sparrows sold for two small coins? Yet not one of them has escaped the notice of God. Even the hairs of your head have all been counted. Do not be afraid. You are worth more than many sparrows.”

The words of the Popes

Jesus sends the disciples out on their mission and (…) he draws a parallel between what we hear “in private,” namely, what is in the secret of our hearts, and what we are called to proclaim to everyone. He reminds us that proclaiming the Gospel is first and foremost a sharing of a personal encounter with him, which is unique to each of us.

The strength of any apostolate, in fact — beyond techniques and tools — comes from the work of the Holy Spirit within us and from the authenticity of our response. Saint Thomas Aquinas spoke of preaching as passing on to others what we have contemplated: “contemplata aliis tradere” (cf. Summa Theologiae, III, q. 40, a. 1, ad 2). (…)

Now, just as then, it is a challenge to remain faithful to Jesus’ teachings and to proclaim his word: to respond to hatred with love, to arrogance with meekness, and to discouragement with perseverance. For this reason, we must deepen the roots of our faith and our mission in an intimate relationship with him (cf. Francis, Apostolic Exhortation Evangelii Gaudium, 8). This gives us the strength not to despair, but to continue to share with everyone, in every circumstance, his message of hope, love and peace. The world greatly needs it! (Pope Leo XIV, Angelus, 21 June 2026)

Word

leaven /ˈlɛvən/ — (noun) a substance, such as yeast, added to dough to make it ferment and rise; figuratively, an influence that spreads through something and changes it from within.

From Middle English levain, from Old French levain, from Vulgar Latin *levāmen, from Latin levāre “to raise, lift.”

Peso closes ₱63.06 — 27th record low, and your import ticket just moved

15 Oct 2026

GMA News: the peso breached ₱63 per dollar on Thursday, closing at ₱63.06 after an intraday low of ₱63.15 — its 27th record low this year and the third straight down session. BusinessMirror and Context.ph put the same close on the board and spell out the operator risk: weaker peso lifts the peso cost of imported inputs, fuel, and materials just as Christmas demand usually rises. Federation of Philippine Industries chair Elizabeth Lee said factories that rely on imported raw materials, components, machinery, and fuel will see production costs climb and pressure prices over time. Operator read: food service sits on that same stack. Yesterday’s desk was about Jollibee and KFC fighting under ₱100; today the currency moves the cost of holding that fight. Practical moves for a Calamba or Metro Manila kitchen — (1) list every SKU priced off a dollar invoice and mark the ₱62→₱63 delta before Monday; (2) freeze new imported LTO experiments until you know the landed cost; (3) prefer local protein and produce lines where the DA still says staples are adequately supplied, and save the FX hit for items guests will actually pay for. Flag: macro FX coverage, not a menu announcement.

USDA: F&B retail only +1.6% this year as guests chase value and smaller packs

6 Oct 2026

PhilSTAR, citing USDA-FAS Manila: Philippine food and beverage retail sales are expected to rise just 1.6% to $123 billion this year from $121 billion, as higher prices soften household consumption and reinforce “price-sensitive, value-seeking” behavior — cost-effective alternatives, essentials first, value over volume. Longer term, USDA still sees about 7% CAGR through 2030 on new retailers, modern chains pushing into cities and rural areas, and e-commerce. The Philippine Amalgamated Supermarkets Association says operators are answering weak spend with smaller, more affordable packs while raising prices on larger packaging to protect margins, and regularly retuning inventory, pack size, and mix to local demographics even as national chains keep expanding. Food and non-alcoholic beverages remain 36% of household spending. Operator read: this is the demand-side twin of the peso story. Guests are not leaving the category; they are downsizing. If your board only offers full meals and large drinks, you are fighting the pack-size war with the wrong SKUs. Build one deliberate small format — solo rice meal, half dessert, 8-oz drink — that still makes money at the new FX, and stop pretending volume will save a thin margin line.
