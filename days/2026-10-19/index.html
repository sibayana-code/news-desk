Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Ephesians 2:1-10

Brothers and sisters: You were dead in your transgressions and sins in which you once lived following the age of this world, following the ruler of the power of the air, the spirit that is now at work in the disobedient. All of us once lived among them in the desires of our flesh, following the wishes of the flesh and the impulses, and we were by nature children of wrath, like the rest. But God, who is rich in mercy, because of the great love he had for us, even when we were dead in our transgressions, brought us to life with Christ (by grace you have been saved), raised us up with him, and seated us with him in the heavens in Christ Jesus, that in the ages to come he might show the immeasurable riches of his grace in his kindness to us in Christ Jesus. For by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not from you; it is the gift of God; it is not from works, so no one may boast. For we are his handiwork, created in Christ Jesus for good works that God has prepared in advance, that we should live in them.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 12:13-21

Someone in the crowd said to Jesus, “Teacher, tell my brother to share the inheritance with me.” He replied to him, “Friend, who appointed me as your judge and arbitrator?” Then he said to the crowd, “Take care to guard against all greed, for though one may be rich, one’s life does not consist of possessions.” Then he told them a parable. “There was a rich man whose land produced a bountiful harvest. He asked himself, ‘What shall I do, for I do not have space to store my harvest?’ And he said, ‘This is what I shall do: I shall tear down my barns and build larger ones. There I shall store all my grain and other goods and I shall say to myself, “Now as for you, you have so many good things stored up for many years, rest, eat, drink, be merry!”’ But God said to him, ‘You fool, this night your life will be demanded of you; and the things you have prepared, to whom will they belong?’ Thus will it be for the one who stores up treasure for himself but is not rich in what matters to God.”

The words of the Popes

The fullness of our existence does not depend on what we store up or, as we heard in the Gospel, on what we possess (cf. Lk 12:13-21). Rather, fullness has to do with what we joyfully welcome and share (cf. Mt 10:8-10; Jn 6:1-13). Buying, hoarding and consuming are not enough. We need to lift our eyes, to look upwards, to the “things that are above” (Col 3:2), to realize that everything in the world has meaning only insofar as it serves to unite us to God and to our brothers and sisters in charity, helping us to grow in “compassion, kindness, humility, meekness and patience” (Col 3:12), forgiveness (cf. ibid., v. 13) and peace (cf. Jn 14:27), all in imitation of Christ (cf. Phil 2:5). And in this way we will grow in an ever deeper understanding of what it means that hope does not disappoint, because the love of God has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us (cf. Rom 5:5). (Pope Leo XIV, Homily, 3 August 2025)

Word

greed /ɡriːd/ — (noun) intense and selfish desire for something, especially wealth, possessions or power, beyond what one needs.

A back-formation from greedy, from Old English grǣdig “hungry, eager,” from Proto-Germanic *grēdagaz.

greed — Luke: “guard against all greed,” because life does not consist of possessions, and the rich fool builds bigger barns the night his life is demanded. Ephesians: we are saved by grace, a gift, not something we earn or store. Leo XIV: buying, hoarding and consuming are not enough; fullness is what we welcome and share. The Way 630: he has most who needs least.

Takes

  • Lemon Square put a store in the same estate as its plant, where 75,000 people already clock in. That’s the cheapest catchment there is: guaranteed weekday traffic, shift breaks you can schedule around, and your own staff as walk-ins. If you’re scouting south of Calamba, rank sites by headcount and shift times in LIMA, Carmelray and LISP, not by census population. Open
  • McDonald’s at ₱59 for the 3 pm slot, Mang Inasal at ₱89 for lunch, Jollibee at ₱99: the big chains now have every daypart covered under ₱100. Don’t fight that at lunch. Fill your own dead afternoon hours with a ₱59–₱79 merienda pair built only from what’s already on the fryer and the drink station, so it adds no new stock.
  • Pork is down about ₱33/kg year on year and still sliding, and storms are quiet. This is the moment to lock pork and vegetable prices through December, before holiday buying lifts them and the El Niño squeeze arrives in January. Mango is the one going the other way, so move December desserts off fresh carabao mango.

Lemon Square plants store number 150 beside its own new factory in LIMA Estate

17 Oct 2026

Daily Tribune: Lemon Square opened its 150th store at The Outlets at LIMA Estate, the Aboitiz Economic Estates development in Batangas. Parent Big E Food Corp. recently launched a manufacturing plant in the Lipa–Malvar area covering 118,132 square meters, or about 11.8 hectares. It’s expected to generate at least 2,000 jobs once fully operational, and it will back up the existing Bulacan plant for the local market and the wider region. Technical director Ernesto Ramos Jr. framed the store as making products more accessible to families around the estate. Aboitiz VP Clifford Academia said it shows how manufacturing and retail can support each other inside one development. LIMA has more than 200 locators and about 75,000 jobs, and it has expanded into offices, retail, housing and schools.

Operator read: this is a snack-bakery brand moving production closer to the fast-growing south and selling right where the workers are. For a Calamba-based opener, two things follow. First, Batangas and southern Laguna are getting more consumer-goods plants, which means more shift workers with steady pay and fixed break times, and those are the easiest diners to forecast. Second, estate outlet strips like The Outlets at LIMA are landlords that want food tenants to keep the workforce on site. Ask for traffic counts by shift and by gate before you look at the rent. Flag: company and estate statements at a ribbon-cutting, no sales or plant output figures.
