Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Philippians 1:1-11

Paul and Timothy, slaves of Christ Jesus, to all the holy ones in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, with the bishops and deacons: grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

I give thanks to my God at every remembrance of you, praying always with joy in my every prayer for all of you, because of your partnership for the Gospel from the first day until now. I am confident of this, that the one who began a good work in you will continue to complete it until the day of Christ Jesus. It is right that I should think this way about all of you, because I hold you in my heart, you who are all partners with me in grace, both in my imprisonment and in the defense and confirmation of the Gospel. For God is my witness, how I long for all of you with the affection of Christ Jesus.

And this is my prayer: that your love may increase ever more and more in knowledge and every kind of perception, to discern what is of value, so that you may be pure and blameless for the day of Christ, filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ for the glory and praise of God.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 14:1-6

On a sabbath Jesus went to dine at the home of one of the leading Pharisees, and the people there were observing him carefully. In front of him there was a man suffering from dropsy. Jesus spoke to the scholars of the law and Pharisees in reply, asking, “Is it lawful to cure on the sabbath or not?” But they kept silent; so he took the man and, after he had healed him, dismissed him. Then he said to them, “Who among you, if your son or ox falls into a cistern, would not immediately pull him out on the sabbath day?” But they were unable to answer his question.

The words of the Popes

Jesus heals: let yourselves be healed by Jesus. We all have wounds, everyone: spiritual wounds, sins, hostility, jealousy; perhaps we don’t say hello to someone: “Ah, he did this to me, I won’t acknowledge him anymore”. But this needs to be healed! “How do I do it?”. Pray and ask that Jesus heal it”. It’s sad in a family when siblings don’t speak to each other for a small matter; because the devil takes a small matter and makes a world of it. Then hostilities go on, often times for many years, and that family is destroyed. Parents suffer because their children don’t speak to each other, or one son’s wife doesn’t speak to the other, and thus, with jealousy, envy.... The devil sows this. And the only One who casts out demons is Jesus. The only One who heals these matters is Jesus. For this reason I say to each one of you: let yourself be healed by Jesus. Each one knows where his wounds are. Each one of us has them; we don’t have only one: two, three, four, 20. Each one knows! May Jesus heal those wounds.

But for this I must open my heart, in order that He may come. How do I open my heart? By praying. “But Lord, I can’t with those people over there. I hate them. They did this, this and this...”. “Heal this wound, Lord”. If we ask Jesus for this grace, He will do it. Let yourself be healed by Jesus. Let Jesus heal you. Let Jesus preach to you and let Him heal you. This way I can even preach to others, to teach the words of Jesus, because I let Him preach to me; and I can also help heal many wounds, the many wounds that there are. But first I have to do it: let Him preach to me and heal me. (Pope Francis, Homily, 8 February 2015)

Word

heal /hiːl/ — (verb) to make sound or whole again; to cure of disease or restore to health; to mend a breach between people.

From Old English hǣlan “to make whole,” from Proto-Germanic *hailijaną; related to whole, hale and holy.

DA: farm-goods prices likely up as the peso hits a record low

29 Oct 2026

GMA News reports the Department of Agriculture expects imported items, rice and fertilizer first, to cost more as the peso sank to an intraday ₱64.18:$1 on Wednesday, past Tuesday’s record close of ₱64.17. Spokesperson Arnel de Mesa said rice prices may rise in November and December despite incoming volumes, citing El Niño. As of Oct 22, 4.36 million metric tons of imported rice had arrived, and DA says key-food inventory, imported and local, covers the rest of 2026. The peso’s slide is tied to crude spiking on the Middle East crisis.

Operator read: “enough supply” and “higher price” are both true here. The cost driver is the exchange rate and fuel, not shortage, so don’t wait for empty shelves to act. Rice is your plate-cost floor: price a December rice buy now, and check imported inputs (cooking oil, flour, dairy, frozen fries) since they ride the same peso. If you’re planning holiday menu prices, build in the move now rather than repricing mid-December.

Vietnam’s Michelin-recognized The Lunch Lady opens in BGC

15 Oct 2026 · opened 29 Oct

Insider PH reports The Lunch Lady, which started as Nguyễn Thị Thành’s Saigon noodle-soup stall in 1995 and was featured by Anthony Bourdain in 2009, opened its first Philippine location on Oct 29 at 102 Forbestown Road, BGC. The brand holds five straight Bib Gourmands in Vancouver (2022–2026) and one in Toronto (2026). The local partner is HTCG Premium Food Concepts Inc., led by Annabelle Chua. Lunch runs rotating Vietnamese noodle soups; dinner shifts to contemporary Southeast Asian plates (Wagyu Phở, Steak Lúc Lắc, Garlic Noodles) with charcoal cooking and craft cocktails.

Operator read: another charcoal-led import into BGC after Torikizoku and Kiwami’s RŌEN this month. Charcoal and grill talent are getting crowded in the south Metro. The format lesson is the split day-part: a cheap, fast noodle lunch for office traffic and a higher-ticket dinner with drinks. That doubles the use of one lease without a second kitchen.
