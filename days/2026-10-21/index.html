Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Ephesians 3:2-12

Brothers and sisters: You have heard of the stewardship of God’s grace that was given to me for your benefit, namely, that the mystery was made known to me by revelation, as I have written briefly earlier. When you read this you can understand my insight into the mystery of Christ, which was not made known to human beings in other generations as it has now been revealed to his holy Apostles and prophets by the Spirit, that the Gentiles are coheirs, members of the same Body, and copartners in the promise in Christ Jesus through the Gospel. Of this I became a minister by the gift of God’s grace that was granted me in accord with the exercise of his power. To me, the very least of all the holy ones, this grace was given, to preach to the Gentiles the inscrutable riches of Christ, and to bring to light for all what is the plan of the mystery hidden from ages past in God who created all things, so that the manifold wisdom of God might now be made known through the Church to the principalities and authorities in the heavens. This was according to the eternal purpose that he accomplished in Christ Jesus our Lord, in whom we have boldness of speech and confidence of access through faith in him.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 12:39-48

Jesus said to his disciples: “Be sure of this: if the master of the house had known the hour when the thief was coming, he would not have let his house be broken into. You also must be prepared, for at an hour you do not expect, the Son of Man will come.” Then Peter said, “Lord, is this parable meant for us or for everyone?” And the Lord replied, “Who, then, is the faithful and prudent steward whom the master will put in charge of his servants to distribute the food allowance at the proper time? Blessed is that servant whom his master on arrival finds doing so. Truly, I say to you, he will put him in charge of all his property. But if that servant says to himself, ‘My master is delayed in coming,’ and begins to beat the menservants and the maidservants, to eat and drink and get drunk, then that servant’s master will come on an unexpected day and at an unknown hour and will punish the servant severely and assign him a place with the unfaithful. That servant who knew his master’s will but did not make preparations nor act in accord with his will shall be beaten severely; and the servant who was ignorant of his master’s will but acted in a way deserving of a severe beating shall be beaten only lightly. Much will be required of the person entrusted with much, and still more will be demanded of the person entrusted with more.”

The words of the Popes

“Every one to whom much is given, of him will much be required; and of him to whom men commit much they will demand the more”. Jesus said it to his closest disciples, those with whom He stayed the most. And we too have received much from the journey we have made so far; we have been with Jesus and with the Church and, even though the Church is a community with human limitations, we have received a great deal. So, Jesus expects a lot of us. It is a sign of trust, a sign of friendship. He expects a lot because He knows us, and knows that we can do it! Jesus came to bring fire: the fire of God’s love on earth and the fire of our hearts’ desire. In a certain way, Jesus takes away our peace, if we think of peace as inert calm. This, however, is not true peace. At times we would like to be “left in peace”: for no-one to disturb us, for others not to exist anymore. This is not God’s peace. The peace Jesus brings is like a fire, and it asks a lot of us. It asks us, first of all, to take a stand. Faced with injustice, inequality, where human dignity is trampled underfoot, where the fragile are silenced: take a stand. To hope is to take a stand. To hope is to understand in our hearts and show in our actions that things must not continue as before. This too is the good fire of the Gospel. (Pope Leo XIV, Jubilee Audience, 22 November 2025)

Word

steward /ˈstuː.ərd/ — (noun) a person who manages another’s property, household or affairs on their behalf.

From Old English stīweard, stigweard, “house guardian,” from stig “house, hall” and weard “guard, keeper.”

DA eyes a temporary stop on pork import clearances to prop up hog farmers

20 Oct 2026

Context.ph: speaking at the Tuesday Club forum in Mandaluyong, Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said a safeguard duty on imported pork is under study, but imports have risen only about 6 to 7% year on year, short of the 25% trigger. So the DA is looking at pausing Sanitary and Phytosanitary Import Clearances for selected pork products, namely kasim, liempo, pigue, jowls and whole pigs. It could start as early as November and last 30 to 60 days, and would only cover shipments not yet paid for or not yet arrived. The DA is waiting on legal advice. It also backs the industry’s proposed ₱210/kg floor for live hogs, but says no law lets it enforce one; some raisers are getting only ₱120 to ₱160/kg. Retail in Metro Manila on Oct 20: ham ₱300, belly ₱370, frozen kasim ₱240, frozen liempo ₱300 per kilo.

Operator read: low hog prices have been the one bit of relief on the cost line, and this is the government signaling it wants them higher. A short pause hits the frozen imported cuts that sit under a lot of sisig, adobo and grill menus, and it would land right as Christmas orders build. Ask your meat supplier for their landed and paid-for stock through December, get a fresh local kasim and liempo quote alongside the frozen one, and don’t reprice the menu until the DA actually signs the order. Flag: proposal only, no order issued.

Super Typhoon Rosal bends north and stays east of Luzon

20 Oct 2026

PAGASA’s 5 pm agriculture warning put Rosal (Champi) 1,270 km east of Central Luzon with 195 km/h winds and gusts to 240 km/h, moving west-northwest at 25 km/h. The forecast track turns it northwest from today, passing closest to extreme northern Luzon on Thursday and Friday, about 425 km east of Basco, Batanes, before it leaves PAR Friday night or early Saturday. It’s expected to stay a super typhoon and may strengthen over the Philippine Sea before weakening Thursday night. GMA’s 4 pm report says its trough brings scattered rain to Bicol and Eastern Visayas today, with rough seas off northern and eastern Luzon and eastern Visayas. Metro Manila gets only isolated thunderstorms.

Operator read: on this track the highland vegetable belt is largely spared, so a big buy-ahead isn’t needed. The real exposure is fishing: rough eastern seas for three or four days can thin Bicol and Visayas catch into Manila markets. If fish is on special this week, check your supplier’s volume before you print it. PAGASA notes the track can still shift inside the cone, so read Friday’s bulletin.
