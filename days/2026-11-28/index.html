Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 21:34-36

Jesus said to his disciples: “Beware that your hearts do not become drowsy from carousing and drunkenness and the anxieties of daily life, and that day catch you by surprise like a trap. For that day will assault everyone who lives on the face of the earth. Be vigilant at all times and pray that you have the strength to escape the tribulations that are imminent and to stand before the Son of Man.”

The words of the Popes

“Beware that your hearts do not become drowsy… Be vigilant at all times and pray” (Lk 21:34, 36). “Be vigilant”: vigilance. Let us focus on this important aspect of the Christian life. From the words of Christ, we see that vigilance is tied to alertness: be alert, do not get distracted, that is, stay awake! Vigilance means this: not to allow our hearts to become lazy or our spiritual life to soften into mediocrity. (…) We need to be vigilant so that our daily life does not become routine, and, as Jesus says, so we are not burdened by life’s anxieties (cf. v. 34). So today is a good moment to ask ourselves: what weighs on my heart? What weighs on my spirit? What makes me go to sit in the lazy chair? It is sad to see Christians “in the armchair”! (…) And let us add an essential ingredient: the secret to being vigilant is prayer. In fact, Jesus says: “Be vigilant at all times and pray” (Lk 21:36). Prayer is what keeps the lamp of the heart lit. This is especially true when we feel that our enthusiasm has cooled down. Prayer re-lights it, because it brings us back to God, to the center of things. Prayer reawakens the soul from sleep and focuses it on what matters, on the purpose of existence. Even during our busiest days, we must not neglect prayer. (Pope Francis, Angelus, 28 November 2021)

Furrow — Prayer

“Your prayer will sometimes be discursive; maybe less often, full of fervour; and, perhaps often, dry, dry, dry. But what matters is that you, with God’s help, are not disheartened. Consider the sentry on duty. He does not know if the King or the Head of State is in the palace: he is not told what he might be doing, and generally the public figure does not know who is on guard. It is not at all like that with our God. He lives where you live, He cares for you and knows your inmost thoughts. Do not abandon the guard-duty of your prayer!”

Word

vigilant /ˈvɪdʒɪlənt/ (adjective) — keeping careful watch for possible danger or difficulty; alert and awake.

From Latin vigilans, present participle of vigilare “to keep awake, watch,” from vigil “awake, watchful.”

Takes

  • This month's inflation comes from your utility lines, not the market. LPG and power are what a kitchen burns all day, so check December's LPG quote and your meter readings before you blame food cost for a thin margin.
  • Onion is up again, now about ₱150. That's three weekly climbs this month. Push tomato- and eggplant-heavy specials while they're cheap and go easy on onion garnish through the holidays.
  • Rice: India's problem doesn't hit your plate tomorrow, but if New Delhi brings back export duties, Vietnam's prices rise and we pay that. Rice is flat today, so this is the time to lock a supplier price into January if you can.

Cheaper at the pump, pricier at home: BSP sees November inflation hitting up to 8.2%

27 Nov 2026 · BNC / Bilyonaryo

The Bangko Sentral expects November inflation between 7.2% and 8.2%. It names higher electricity rates and cooking gas as the upward push. Lower local pump prices could ease some of it, and it says good supply could bring down rice, vegetable, meat and fish prices. BSP says it is also watching the Middle East.

Operator read: diners feel 8% inflation as smaller checks and fewer add-ons, while your LPG and power bills rise at the same time. Don't do a blanket menu price hike. Protect the value tier, re-price the items that eat the most gas (fryers, long braises), and run any oven or fryer that sits idle off-peak at lower settings. The official November number is due next week.

Why India's rising rice prices matter to Filipinos: Seven takeaways

27 Nov 2026 · Reuters via Bilyonaryo

India's average rice price rose 12.5% year on year in October even with record government stocks of 55.47 million tonnes. Those stocks are mostly common grades that don't replace the premium varieties consumers want. A weak monsoon means India's crop is expected to fall for the first time in a decade, and feed and ethanol makers are bidding for broken rice. India supplies nearly 40% of world rice trade. New Delhi could bring back a 20% duty on non-basmati exports, minimum export prices, or a ban on broken-rice exports. Vietnam, Thailand and Pakistan would gain share, and the Philippines buys mainly from Vietnam.

Operator read: an Indian curb lets Vietnam raise its prices, and our import price follows a few weeks later. Rice is unli on a lot of PH menus, so it's the cost you can't easily pass on. Ask your supplier for a fixed price through Q1, and know what a ₱2–3/kg rise does to an unli-rice plate before it happens.
