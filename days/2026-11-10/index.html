Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to Titus 2:1-8, 11-14

Beloved: You must say what is consistent with sound doctrine, namely, that older men should be temperate, dignified, self-controlled, sound in faith, love, and endurance. Similarly, older women should be reverent in their behavior, not slanderers, not addicted to drink, teaching what is good, so that they may train younger women to love their husbands and children, to be self-controlled, chaste, good homemakers, under the control of their husbands, so that the word of God may not be discredited. Urge the younger men, similarly, to control themselves, showing yourself as a model of good deeds in every respect, with integrity in your teaching, dignity, and sound speech that cannot be criticized, so that the opponent will be put to shame without anything bad to say about us. Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 17:7-10

Jesus said to the Apostles: “Who among you would say to your servant who has just come in from plowing or tending sheep in the field, ‘Come here immediately and take your place at table’? Would he not rather say to him, ‘Prepare something for me to eat. Put on your apron and wait on me while I eat and drink. You may eat and drink when I am finished’? Is he grateful to that servant because he did what was commanded? So should it be with you. When you have done all you have been commanded, say, ‘We are unprofitable servants; we have done what we were obliged to do.’”

The words of the Popes

Jesus invites us to be humble and suggests the example of a servant who has worked in the fields. When he returns home, the master asks him to go on working. According to the mentality of Jesus' time the master had every right to do this. The servant owed his master total availability; and the master did not feel under any obligation to him for having carried out the orders he had received. Jesus makes us aware that, before God, we are in a similar situation: we are God's servants, we are not his creditors but are always indebted to him, because we owe him everything since everything is a gift from him. Accepting and doing his will is the approach to have every day, at every moment of our life. Before God we must never present ourselves as if we believe we have done a service and deserve a great reward. This is an illusion that can be born in everyone, even in people who work very hard in the Lord's service, in the Church. Rather, we must be aware that in reality we never do enough for God. We must say, as Jesus' suggests: "we are unworthy servants; we have only done what was our duty" (Lk 17: 10). This is an attitude of humility that really puts us in our place and permits the Lord to be very generous to us. (Pope Benedict XVI, Homily, Foro Italico in Palermo, 3 October 2010)

Takes

  • Twenty percent fewer staff is the number that matters with wages rising. You don't need robots to get some of that: look at QR ordering at the table, runner-free pass layouts and fewer handoffs, and count the heads each change saves. Open
  • Chowking pushing direct-app ordering with cheaper delivery is Jollibee Group pulling margin back from Grab and foodpanda. If your delivery mix is big, a simple direct-order channel (even Messenger plus your own riders in Calamba) is worth costing out. Open

Customer experience over franchising: Restaurant group expands Japanese dining concept

10 Nov 2026 · The Freeman (Philstar)

Cebu's Nonki Group has opened its third and largest Sushi Station at Ayala Center Cebu, its first Ayala Malls site after 19 years in SM City Cebu. It follows branches at J Mall and SM City Davao. Instead of a conveyor belt, the concept uses sensor-guided delivery robots that carry food to tables, which COO Junichi Machida says lets it run with about 20% fewer employees than a comparable Nonki restaurant. Despite that, the group has no plans to franchise Sushi Station, saying fast growth would hurt food and service consistency.

Operator read: this is labor-cost engineering dressed as a gimmick. The robots draw the crowd, but the real win is fewer runners on the payroll as wages, logistics and ingredient costs rise together. Staying company-owned also matters: a format that depends on tight execution travels badly through franchisees. If you're opening something new, design the floor and the ordering flow to need fewer people before you think about decor. And note that a 19-year SM tenant moved to Ayala for its flagship, so mall operators are competing for proven local concepts.

Agri dept sees price hikes in vegetables as holiday season nears

9 Nov 2026 · ABS-CBN News

The Department of Agriculture says prices are mostly stable, except for lowland vegetables such as eggplant, tomato and kalamansi, which rose about 20% from the previous week. Highland crops such as carrots and cabbage also went up. Assistant Secretary Genevieve Velicaria-Guevarra called it the normal seasonal pattern before the holidays and said the DA is stepping up monitoring at trading posts in both highland and lowland areas.

Operator read: "normal" doesn't make it cheap. With El Niño expected to peak before year end and last into Q1, this is the start of the climb, not a blip. Fix December prices on your top vegetable lines this week, keep kalamansi and tomato use measured in recipes, and price holiday trays and bundles with that in mind rather than at today's cost.
