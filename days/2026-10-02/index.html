Reading of the day

A reading from the Book of Job 38:1, 12-21; 40:3-5

The LORD addressed Job out of the storm and said:

Have you ever in your lifetime commanded the morning and shown the dawn its place For taking hold of the ends of the earth, till the wicked are shaken from its surface? The earth is changed as is clay by the seal, and dyed as though it were a garment; But from the wicked the light is withheld, and the arm of pride is shattered.

Have you entered into the sources of the sea, or walked about in the depths of the abyss? Have the gates of death been shown to you, or have you seen the gates of darkness? Tell me, if you know all: Which is the way to the dwelling place of light, and where is the abode of darkness, That you may take them to their boundaries and set them on their homeward paths? Behold, I am of little account; what can I answer you? I put my hand over my mouth. Though I have spoken once, I will not do so again; though twice, I will do so no more.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Matthew 18:1-5, 10

The disciples approached Jesus and said, “Who is the greatest in the Kingdom of heaven?” He called a child over, placed it in their midst, and said, “Amen, I say to you, unless you turn and become like children, you will not enter the Kingdom of heaven. Whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest in the Kingdom of heaven. And whoever receives one child such as this in my name receives me.

“See that you do not despise one of these little ones, for I say to you that their angels in heaven always look upon the face of my heavenly Father.”

The words of the Popes

According to the tradition of the Church, we all have an angel with us, who watches over us and helps us to sense things. How many times have we heard: “But … this … I should do it this way, this will not do, be careful …”: so many times! And that is the voice of our travelling companion. (…) And when we do not want to heed his advice, to listen to his voice, it is like telling him: “Go away!”. Driving away our travelling companion is perilous, because no man, no woman can advise themselves. I can advise another person, but I cannot advise myself. There is the Holy Spirit who advises me, there is the angel who advises me. That is why we need them. This is not some fanciful doctrine about angels: no, it is reality. What Jesus, what God said was: “I will send an angel before you to watch over you, to accompany you on your journey, so that you do not go astray”. (…) Today, I would ask the question: how is my relationship with my guardian angel? Do I listen to him? Do I say “good morning” to him in the morning? Do I say, “Watch over me while I sleep”? Do I speak with him? Do I ask him for advice? He is by my side. We can answer this question today, each one of us: how is my relationship with this angel that the Lord has sent to watch over me and to accompany me on my journey, and who always sees the face of the Father who is in heaven? (Pope Francis, Homily at Santa Marta, 2 October 2014)

The Way — Spiritual childhood

“Don't try to be grown-up. A child, always a child, even when you are dying of old age. When a child stumbles and falls, nobody is surprised; his father promptly lifts him up. When the person who stumbles and falls is older, the immediate reaction is one of laughter. Sometimes this first impulse passes and the laughter gives way to pity. BSP: September inflation may hit a three-year high on weather food

1 Oct 2026

Philstar (Keisha Ta-Asan): Bangko Sentral says September headline inflation likely landed between 6.4% and 7.4%, up from August’s 6.1%. The upper end would be the fastest since March 2023’s 7.6%. Even the low end keeps inflation above the 2–4% target for a seventh straight month. PSA releases the official print Monday, Oct 6. BSP’s named upward pressures: weather-related lifts in vegetables, fish, rice, and fruit; higher domestic petroleum; peso depreciation that raises import costs for fuel, food, and raws. Named offsets: lower meat prices and electricity. RCBC’s Michael Ricafort centers 6.7% and warns a strong El Niño could keep pressing rice and other food into early 2027, with second-round effects via the ₱60 Metro Manila daily minimum-wage hike and energy/import pass-through. BSP has already lifted the policy rate 75bp to 5% since April. Operator read for Calamba: this is yesterday’s PSA kitchen receipt upgraded to a macro threat. Rice at ~₱56 and +19% YoY is not a one-week blip — it is the floor under every rice meal and every QSR value stack into Q4. Do not rewrite fixed lunch prices before Oct 6; do float highland/lowland veg specials and dual-source greens. Soft meat that “offset” September is the same soft pork DA wants to reverse with 2027 tariff steps — treat cheap protein as a hedge window, not a new baseline. If Ricafort’s second-round call lands, wage and logistics will reprice your ticket even if commodity prints ease.

FAO: PH reclaims #2 banana exporter — +30% to 3 MMT

26 Sep 2026 (2025 trade year)

BusinessMirror / FAO: The Philippines reclaimed the world’s second-largest banana-exporter rank in 2025 after shipments jumped 30% to 3 million metric tons from 2.33 MMT in 2024, reversing the slide that left Guatemala ahead after 2022. FAO credits better TR4 (Fusarium Wilt Tropical Race 4) management, easing civil unrest, and “substantial” production investments in Cagayan Valley — including DA farm inputs and fertilizers. China and Japan remain the key markets; PH still supplies 75–80% of Japan’s banana imports, though Japan’s take from Vietnam rose 38%. On China quotes, Vietnam averaged ~$407/MT versus ~$600 for Philippine and Cambodian fruit (Ecuador ~$755). FAO flags the usual export-risk stack: conflict, fertilizer/energy/transport shocks, high living costs and rates. Operator read: this is an agri-export win, not a cheaper local Cavendish plate for Calamba kitchens. What matters for an opener is the signal — Mindanao/Cagayan Valley supply recovering under disease discipline and DA input support. That same discipline is what you want on any protein or produce line you dual-source. Vietnam’s China price undercut is the competitive reminder: volume reclaim does not equal pricing power. For kitchen planning, keep bananas as garnish/dessert attach, not a cost thesis; for entrepreneurial read, watch which packers translate TR4 control into steady weekly export cadence — those are the operators worth knowing when you need reliable tropical fruit into a menu or a commissary.
