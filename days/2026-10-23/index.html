Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 12:54-59

Jesus said to the crowds, “When you see a cloud rising in the west you say immediately that it is going to rain–and so it does; and when you notice that the wind is blowing from the south you say that it is going to be hot–and so it is. You hypocrites! You know how to interpret the appearance of the earth and the sky; why do you not know how to interpret the present time? “Why do you not judge for yourselves what is right? If you are to go with your opponent before a magistrate, make an effort to settle the matter on the way; otherwise your opponent will turn you over to the judge, and the judge hand you over to the constable, and the constable throw you into prison. I say to you, you will not be released until you have paid the last penny.”

The words of the Popes

Knowing the signs of the times. Times change, and it is in the nature of Christian wisdom to recognize these changes, to recognize the different times and to recognize the signs of the times. To understand what one thing means and what another means. And to do this without fear, with freedom. This is a task we usually don’t do. Instead, we conform, we become tranquillized with “they told me; I heard; people say; I read...”, and we remain comfortable. Instead, we should ask ourselves: “What is the truth? What is the message that the Lord wants to give me with that sign of the times?”. First and foremost, silence is necessary: to be quiet, to watch and observe. And afterwards to think within ourselves. For example: why are there so many wars now? Why did this thing happen? And to pray: silence, reflection and prayer. Only then can we understand the signs of the times and what Jesus wants to tell us. Times change and we Christians must continually change. We must change, steadfast in the faith in Jesus Christ, steadfast in the truth of the Gospel, but our approach must constantly move according to the signs of the times. (Pope Francis, Homily at Santa Marta, 23 October 2015)

El Niño farm damage tops ₱8.57B; corn takes ₱5B

21 Oct 2026

GMA News: the Department of Agriculture said El Niño damage and losses have reached ₱8.57 billion, with 288,058 farmers affected and 291,154 hectares of farmland destroyed across 14 regions, Calabarzon among them. Corn took the biggest share at ₱5 billion, rice ₱2.95 billion, and high-value crops such as vegetables, spices, legumes, watermelon, banana and pineapple ₱571.28 million. Livestock losses were small at ₱37.75 million. The DA says it has given ₱11.34 billion in interventions, mostly seeds and fertilizer, has started fuel subsidies for fisherfolk, and will begin subsidies for farmers using machinery in late October or November. The numbers are still being validated.

Operator read: rice prices have been easing, so the ₱2.95B rice hit is not a menu problem this quarter. Corn is the one to watch, because it feeds the chickens and hogs you buy in 2027. Add the DA’s push for higher pork tariffs and the signal is the same as yesterday: hold today’s prices on the menu, lock holiday protein volume now, and budget chicken and pork higher for next year. Separately, the government’s closed fishing season runs November to February, with imported fish filling the gap, so plan seafood specials around imported species and check prices weekly through the holidays.

Jollibee brings the Dubai chocolate craze to a ₱79 Christmas sundae (promo)

19 Oct 2026

Insider PH: Jollibee Philippines teamed up with Dubai-based Filipino pastry chef Nouel Catis on the Pistachio Choco Sundae, vanilla soft serve in a pistachio shell with chocolate syrup and diced pistachios. It costs ₱79 and runs nationwide from October 16 to December 31 across dine-in, takeout, drive-thru, GrabFood and foodpanda. Marketing VP Dorothy Ching framed it as new flavors for the season while spotlighting Filipino culinary talent.

Operator read: this is a promo, flagged as such, but the play is worth noting. Jollibee is not discounting its core meals this Christmas; it is adding a cheap, trendy dessert that raises the average check and gets people talking. A run that ends December 31 keeps it a seasonal item, not a menu commitment. If you open or run a dessert or café concept, a single pistachio item with a named-chef or local-maker story can do the same job without a permanent menu change.
