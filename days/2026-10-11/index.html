Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Matthew 22:1-14

Jesus again in reply spoke to the chief priests and elders of the people in parables, saying, "The kingdom of heaven may be likened to a king who gave a wedding feast for his son. He dispatched his servants to summon the invited guests to the feast, but they refused to come. A second time he sent other servants, saying, 'Tell those invited: "Behold, I have prepared my banquet, my calves and fattened cattle are killed, and everything is ready; come to the feast."' Some ignored the invitation and went away, one to his farm, another to his business. The rest laid hold of his servants, mistreated them, and killed them. The king was enraged and sent his troops, destroyed those murderers, and burned their city. Then he said to his servants, 'The feast is ready, but those who were invited were not worthy to come. Go out, therefore, into the main roads and invite to the feast whomever you find.' The servants went out into the streets and gathered all they found, bad and good alike, and the hall was filled with guests. But when the king came in to meet the guests, he saw a man there not dressed in a wedding garment. The king said to him, 'My friend, how is it that you came in here without a wedding garment?' But he was reduced to silence. Then the king said to his attendants, 'Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the darkness outside, where there will be wailing and grinding of teeth.' Many are invited, but few are chosen."

The words of the Popes

Today’s Gospel passage tells us about a king who prepares a wedding banquet for his son (cf. Mt 22:1-14). He is a powerful man, but he is above all a generous father, who invites others to share in his joy. In particular, he reveals the goodness of his heart in the fact that he does not compel anyone, but invites everyone, even though this way exposes him to the possibility of rejection. We notice that he prepares a banquet, freely offering an opportunity for encounter, an opportunity for a feast. This is what God prepares for us: a banquet, to be in communion with him and among ourselves. And we, all of us, are therefore God’s invited guests. But a wedding banquet requires time and commitment on our part: it requires a “yes”: to go, to go to the Lord’s invitation. He invites, but he leaves us free.

This is the type of relationship that the Father offers us: he calls us to stay with him, leaving us the possibility to accept or not accept [the invitation]. He does not propose to us a relationship of subjection, but rather of fatherhood and sonship, which is necessarily conditioned by our free assent. God is very respectful regarding freedom; very respectful. Saint Augustine uses a very beautiful expression in this regard, saying: “He who created us without our help will not save us without our consent” (Sermonclxix, 13). And certainly not because he does not have the capacity to do so — God is omnipotent! — but because, being love, he respects our freedom fully. God proposes: he does not impose. Never. (Pope Francis, Angelus, 15 October 2023)

DA: pork as low as ₱240 in places, and that worries them more than it pleases them

10 Oct 2026

UNTV: Agriculture Undersecretary Asis Perez said pork that sold for more than ₱400 a kilo is now in the ₱300s in some markets and as low as ₱240–250 in others. Pork production rose in the first and second quarters against last year, so prices may stay soft. Perez said DA has to balance this, because raisers who lose money stop producing, and then prices climb again. On rice, he called the recent rise a correction from last year’s unusually low prices rather than inflation. He pointed instead to input costs: fuel has nearly doubled, fertilizer is up more than 30%, labor is up, and farmers now need ₱10,000 to ₱15,000 a hectare in working capital. DA said it sees no shortage risk for the holidays and projects that stocks on Dec 31 will cover 170 days of pork, 125 days of chicken and 85 days of rice. It is also pushing continued ASF vaccination to protect hog output. Operator read: pork is the cheapest it has been all year, and every signal says this won’t last. The government is saying out loud that it wants farmgate prices up, hog groups are lobbying for tariffs back at 30–40%, and losses are pushing small raisers out. Use the window. Sign Q1 volume with your meat supplier at current levels, move pork-heavy dishes into December promos where the margin is real, and don’t build a permanent menu price around ₱250 pork. The 85-day rice cover is the thinnest of the three, so watch rice-included meal pricing first.

El Niño peak pushed to January; DA still sees farm output shrinking into early 2027

10 Oct 2026

DA and Manila Times: Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said the dry-spell peak is now expected in January rather than November, so the hit may be milder than first feared. “No need to panic. We have enough food supply,” he said. He still expects farm output to contract through the first quarter of 2027 because of southwest monsoon damage and the coming drought. PAGASA projects a very strong El Niño by year-end that runs through the first half of 2027. DA is rolling out solar irrigation, solar pumps and pond aerators, plus seeds and fertilizer, and expanding Kadiwa direct-to-consumer selling by farmer co-ops. Farm groups are less calm. KMP, citing DA figures, puts damage so far at ₱5.56 billion across 223,497 hectares, with 41,164 hectares a total loss. It says DA expects palay output to fall by up to 700,000 tonnes, and that only ₱6.2 billion of a ₱45 billion aid program has been released. Operator read: the risk hasn’t gone away, it has moved into Q1. That helps December, because the holiday rush runs on current stocks. It also means January and February are when highland vegetables, fish and palay get squeezed, just as post-holiday traffic drops. Plan for it now. Build a lower-vegetable alternate spec for your top sellers, line up a second vegetable supplier outside Benguet, and schedule any price increase for late January instead of reacting in March. For a Laguna operator, Kadiwa co-ops are worth a sourcing call while they’re looking for buyers.
