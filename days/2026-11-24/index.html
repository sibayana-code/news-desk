From the Gospel according to Luke 21:5-11

While some people were speaking about how the temple was adorned with costly stones and votive offerings, Jesus said, “All that you see here– the days will come when there will not be left a stone upon another stone that will not be thrown down.” Then they asked him, “Teacher, when will this happen? The words of the Popes

Today’s Gospel (…) invites us to reflect on the travails of history and the end times. Looking at these events and knowing our hearts, Jesus invites us not to be overcome by fear: “When you hear of wars and insurrections,” he says, “do not be terrified” (v. 9). His appeal is very timely because unfortunately we receive daily news about conflicts, disasters and persecutions that torment millions of men and women. However, in the face of these afflictions, and in the face of the indifference that seeks to ignore them, Jesus’ words proclaim that the attack of evil cannot destroy the hope of those who trust in him. The darker the hour, the more faith shines like the sun. (…) Jesus’ words, in their prophetic way, attest to the fact that the disasters and sorrows of history will come to an end. At the same time, the joy of those who recognize him as Savior, is destined to last forever. (Pope Leo XIV, Angelus, 16 November 2025)

DA says pork and chicken hold through Christmas but vegetables won't, and a Santa Rosa inasal comes back from a fire by way of a food truck

Tape

  • Proteins · NCR Bantay Presyo, 22 Nov: pork belly average ₱368.86/kg (₱300–440), whole chicken ₱199.92/kg, tilapia ₱120–200, bangus ₱180–280. DA expects pork and chicken to stay stable through the holidays. (Daily Tribune, 23 Nov)
  • Vegetables · Tomatoes up to ₱170/kg, carrots ₱180, eggplant ₱160, some cabbage ₱150. Tiu Laurel flags El Niño as the weak spot. (Daily Tribune, 23 Nov)
  • Rice · eAgri NCR well-milled local at ₱48.83/kg on 22 Nov, down from ₱49.14 on 20 Nov. DA says ₱65 imported premium is Japanese and basmati, not a broad rise.
  • Laguna pipeline · SM Nuvali opens Fri 27 Nov, three days out. Aidalicious Chicken Inasal reopens in Santa Rosa before December (story below).

Takes

  • Lock your December pork and chicken now while DA is on record that prices are stable. Your holiday risk is the vegetable line: write side dishes and garnishes so you can swap tomato and carrot out without reprinting the menu.
  • The Aidalicious comeback is a business-continuity lesson for any grill concept: insurance did not cover the mall back rent. Check what your policy pays for business interruption and rent, and know where a backup grill or truck could legally run within 30 days.
  • Santa Rosa inasal gets busier: Aidalicious goes into Paseo Outlet facing Kanin Club, the same week SM Nuvali opens. If you're scouting the area for an inasal format, price your plate against them on day one.

DA: holiday pork, chicken and fish should hold; El Niño puts vegetables at risk

23 Nov 2026 · Daily Tribune

Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said Monday there is enough pork and chicken to prevent big price jumps over Christmas, and that local tilapia and milkfish production keeps fish manageable even with the closed fishing season pushing more imports (imported galunggong ₱260–300/kg). Vegetables are the exception: he said prices "might fluctuate slightly because of El Niño," and DA monitoring already shows tomatoes up to ₱170/kg and carrots up to ₱180. On rice, he said ₱65/kg imported premium reflects specialty varieties, and regular local rice still sells at ₱38–52.

Operator read: this is the window to fix proteins. A minister on record saying "stable" is about as good as forward guidance gets in this market, so negotiate fixed December prices with your pork and chicken suppliers this week. Vegetables are where Q1 margin leaks, after El Niño already cut farm output. Cost your plates at today's ₱170 tomato, not October's, and line up substitutes (cucumber, sayote, pechay) your cooks can switch to without a staff memo.

Burned out in July, Aidalicious Chicken Inasal reopens in Santa Rosa after months on a food truck

23 Nov 2026 · PEP.ph

Say Alonzo and husband Ryan Tordesillas lost their Aidalicious Chicken Inasal by Aida's Bacolod restaurant to a fire on 25 July. They first grilled from home, which Alonzo said mostly lost money and drew a neighbor's complaint, then moved to a food truck at Vista Mall Santa Rosa beside Jollibee. There, pickleball players and Grab orders keep 13 staff on payroll. The restaurant reopens before December at Paseo Outlet, Santa Rosa, opposite Kanin Club and next to Café Breton. Alonzo said the original capital was her husband's retirement money and that the insurance payout was not enough, because they still owed the mall back rent.

Operator read: this is your category in your town. Three things to take from it. First, a truck at a mall with an anchor nearby (pickleball, Jollibee) plus delivery apps kept the brand alive and the staff paid until a new site was ready. That is a cheap bridge format worth keeping on file. Second, insurance that pays for the fire but not the rent you still owe is a gap many operators don't know they have. Third, a celebrity-linked inasal opening at Paseo, close to Nuvali, means more competition for the chicken plate in Santa Rosa in Q1. Expect promo noise at launch.
