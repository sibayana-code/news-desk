Reading of the day

A reading from the Book of Ezekiel 47:1-2, 8-9, 12

The angel brought me back to the entrance of the temple, and I saw water flowing out from beneath the threshold of the temple toward the east, for the façade of the temple was toward the east; the water flowed down from the southern side of the temple, south of the altar. He led me outside by the north gate, and around to the outer gate facing the east, where I saw water trickling from the southern side. He said to me, "This water flows into the eastern district down upon the Arabah, and empties into the sea, the salt waters, which it makes fresh. Wherever the river flows, every sort of living creature that can multiply shall live, and there shall be abundant fish, for wherever this water comes the sea shall be made fresh. Along both banks of the river, fruit trees of every kind shall grow; their leaves shall not fade, nor their fruit fail. Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to John 2:13-22

Since the Passover of the Jews was near, Jesus went up to Jerusalem. He found in the temple area those who sold oxen, sheep, and doves, as well as the money-changers seated there. He made a whip out of cords and drove them all out of the temple area, with the sheep and oxen, and spilled the coins of the money-changers and overturned their tables, and to those who sold doves he said, "Take these out of here, and stop making my Father's house a marketplace." His disciples recalled the words of Scripture, Zeal for your house will consume me. At this the Jews answered and said to him, "What sign can you show us for doing this?" Jesus answered and said to them, "Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up." The Jews said, "This temple has been under construction for forty-six years, and you will raise it up in three days?" But he was speaking about the temple of his Body. Therefore, when he was raised from the dead, his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they came to believe the Scripture and the word Jesus had spoken.

The words of the Popes

In order to better understand today’s Gospel passage, we must highlight an important characteristic. The merchants were in the courtyard reserved for Gentiles, the area that was accessible to non Jews. This very hall had been transformed into a market. But God wants his temple to be “a house of prayer for all peoples” (Is 56:7).(…) Jesus knows that this provocative behaviour will cost him dear. And when they ask him: “What sign have you to show us for doing this?” (Jn 2:18), the Lord answers by saying: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up” (Jn 2:19). (…) In our lives as sinners, we often happen to distance ourselves from the Lord and to dampen the Spirit. We destroy the temple of God which is each of us. And yet this is never a definitive situation: three days are enough for the Lord to rebuild his temple within us! As much as they may have been wounded by evil, no one is condemned to be forever separated from God on this earth. In an often mysterious but real way, the Lord opens new small openings in hearts, the desire for truth, good and beauty that make room for evangelization. At times, one can come across mistrust and hostility. One should not allow oneself to be stopped but rather safeguard the conviction that for God three days are enough to resuscitate his Son in the heart of man. (Pope Francis, Homily, 9 November 2019)

Word

basilica /bəˈsɪlɪkə/ (noun) — a large, important church, often given special rights by the Pope; originally a Roman public hall used for law courts and assemblies.

From Latin basilica, from Greek basilikē (stoa) “royal (portico),” from basilikos “kingly,” from basileus “king.” Related to basil, “the royal herb.”

Eating out is getting dearer faster than groceries as the NCR wage hike and power costs reach the menu

Tape

  • PSA, October · restaurant and accommodation services inflation rose to 7.7% from 7.0%, while food and non-alcoholic drinks eased to 6.1% from 6.7%. PSA's Mapa blames energy, food and raw-material costs plus the NCR wage hike. (BusinessWorld, 5 Nov)
  • NCR minimum wage · up ₱60 since 26 Sep: ₱755 a day for non-agricultural workers, ₱718 for retail, service and small manufacturing. (BusinessWorld, 5 Nov)
  • Rice · local regular milled averaged ₱49.87/kg in October, up 24.17% on the year; well-milled ₱56.19/kg. Mapa says El Niño is now feeding into palay output. (BusinessWorld, 5 Nov)
  • DA · keeping fuel and toll subsidies and lower tariffs on feed corn, and widening the ₱20/kg Benteng Bigas program to seniors, solo parents and PWDs. Corn inflation 21.5%, fruit 10.8%, meat down 3.6% for the poorest 30% of households. (DA, 5 Nov)

Takes

  • When restaurant prices outrun grocery prices, customers notice the gap and cook at home more. Push value you control, like rice refills, bundled sides and a cheaper weekday set, rather than another across-the-board menu hike. Open
  • Meat is the one cheap input right now while rice and corn run hot. Lean on pork and chicken in December specials and keep rice portions measured, not eyeballed. Open

Inflation surges to near 4-year high in October

5 Nov 2026 · BusinessWorld

Headline inflation hit 7.8% in October, the fastest since February 2023, as energy costs spilled into transport, utilities and restaurant services. Dining-out inflation picked up to 7.7% from 7.0%, which PSA chief Dennis Mapa put down to second-round effects of energy, food and raw-material costs and the ₱60 NCR minimum wage hike that took effect on 26 September. Food at home cooled to 6.1% on better weather and supply, but rice kept climbing, with local regular milled up 24% on the year to ₱49.87 a kilo. Mapa warned that El Niño is now feeding through to food prices.

Operator read: this is the squeeze in one line. Your labor, power and rice lines are all rising at once, and your guests can see that a grocery basket is rising more slowly than your menu. Calamba sits outside the NCR wage order, so a Laguna kitchen has a real labor-cost edge over a Makati one right now; that is an argument for commissary or prep work south of Manila. On pricing, raise selectively on items where you lead the market and hold the entry-price items that drive traffic. Rice is the item to watch: at ₱50 a kilo and rising into El Niño, unlimited-rice offers need a hard look before Q1.
