Reading of the day

A reading from the Letter of St. Paul to the Galatians 5:1-6

Brothers and sisters: For freedom Christ set us free; so stand firm and do not submit again to the yoke of slavery. It is I, Paul, who am telling you that if you have yourselves circumcised, Christ will be of no benefit to you. Once again I declare to every man who has himself circumcised that he is bound to observe the entire law. You are separated from Christ, you who are trying to be justified by law; you have fallen from grace. For through the Spirit, by faith, we await the hope of righteousness. For in Christ Jesus, neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but only faith working through love.

Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 11:37-41

After Jesus had spoken, a Pharisee invited him to dine at his home. He entered and reclined at table to eat. The Pharisee was amazed to see that he did not observe the prescribed washing before the meal. The Lord said to him, “Oh you Pharisees! Although you cleanse the outside of the cup and the dish, inside you are filled with plunder and evil. You fools! Did not the maker of the outside also make the inside? But as to what is within, give alms, and behold, everything will be clean for you.”

The words of the Popes

Freedom is a treasure that is truly appreciated only when it is lost. For many of us who are used to living in freedom, it often appears to be an acquired right rather than a gift and a legacy to be preserved. How many misunderstandings there are around the topic of freedom, and how many different views have clashed over the centuries!

In the case of the Galatians, the Apostle could not bear that those Christians, after having known and accepted the truth of Christ, allowed themselves to be attracted to deceptive proposals, moving from freedom to slavery: from the liberating presence of Jesus to slavery to sin, to legalism, and so forth. Even today, legalism is one of our problems for many Christians who take refuge in legalism, in sophistry. Paul therefore invites the Christians to remain firm in the freedom they had received in baptism, without allowing themselves to be put once again under the “yoke of slavery” (Gal 5:1). He is rightly protective of this freedom. (…) A proclamation that would preclude freedom in Christ would never be evangelical. It might be Pelagian or Jansenist or something like that, but not evangelical. One can never force in the name of Jesus; one cannot make anyone a slave in the name of Jesus who makes us free. Freedom is a gift which was given to us at baptism. (Pope Francis, General Audience, 6 October 2021)

Word

freedom /ˈfriːdəm/ — (noun) the state of being free; the power to act, speak or choose without being held under another’s control; release from slavery or bondage.

From Old English frēodōm, from frēo “free” + -dōm “state, condition.” Related to German frei; the root first meant “dear, beloved,” since free members of a household were the loved ones, not the slaves.

freedom — Galatians: for freedom Christ set us free, so do not take up the yoke again; only faith working through love counts. Luke: the Pharisee washes the outside of the cup. Francis: legalism trades freedom for slavery; freedom is a gift of baptism. Furrow 787: never so free as when freedom is woven with love and trust in God. Epictetus: no one is free who is not master of himself.

Shakey’s brings in a Century Pacific executive as COO and calls 2026 its cleanup year

2 Oct 2026

Philstar: Shakey’s Pizza Asia Ventures Inc. (SPAVI), which runs Shakey’s, Potato Corner, Peri-Peri Charcoal Chicken and Sauce Bar, R&B Milk Tea and Project Pie, told the stock exchange it is streamlining its leadership to “strengthen execution.” Edwin Raymond Africa became group COO on Oct 1. He spent 11 years at Century Pacific Food, most recently as executive vice president and group business unit head, after 22 years at Procter & Gamble, Nippon Paint and PepsiCo. He will run the combined Potato Corner business, at home and abroad, plus several corporate functions. Yiow Tan, who leads the international business, stays until the end of 2026, and then that role goes away. Potato Corner COO Roy Marvin Quejada moves to run Peri-Peri, whose general manager is leaving. Chairman Christopher Po has called 2026 a restructuring year: “We’re really taking this as an opportunity, not letting a good crisis go to waste and restructuring. Closing underperforming stores, streamlining our corporate support offices, etc., so that when the crisis is over, we will have done the streamlining, and then our margins would return to what they were before.” The network stood at 3,074 units at mid-year. Operator read: one of the biggest multi-brand groups is choosing to shrink and tighten now rather than wait for sales to come back. Two things follow. First, closures by big groups free up proven mall and strip sites, so watch for vacated Shakey’s and kiosk spots in your area and ask landlords early. Second, putting an FMCG executive over Potato Corner signals that the kiosk brand will be run more like a packaged-goods business, with tighter distribution and fewer, stronger formats. If you compete in snacks or kiosks, expect sharper pricing and promos from them in 2027.

DA: rice won’t be forced cheaper, and the fix it wants is cheaper power and logistics

12 Oct 2026

Manila Bulletin, via AgriInsite: Speaking after 29 international joint-venture agreements were signed at the ASEAN Food Security and Agribusiness Summit in Manila on Saturday, DA Undersecretary for Policy Asis Perez pushed back on calls for cheaper rice. He said Philippine rice prices are already “way below” those of Asian neighbors and that Filipinos spend about 6.7 to 7% of average income on rice, against 25% in India. “Whenever we talk about prices, we always want prices to go down when everybody is asking salaries to go up,” he said. He wants government to target farmers’ and agribusinesses’ costs instead, especially electricity tariffs and waste in processing and distribution. PSA figures show the pressure is real: rice inflation reached 20.3% in September, up from 19.4% in August, and 23.6% for the poorest 30% of households, whose food inflation hit 9.8%. Separately, UNTV (Oct 10) quoted Perez calling the rice price rise a correction from last year’s lows, and noted that farmers’ fuel costs have nearly doubled and fertilizer is up over 30%. Operator read: don’t plan on rice getting cheaper this quarter. DA is defending current prices, and the Manila Times reports retail rice is steady, so the cost is high but predictable. Budget it as a fixed input, look at portion and pricing on rice-heavy combos, and keep unlimited-rice offers to dayparts where they actually drive traffic. Your customers are feeling 20% rice inflation at home, so a strong rice-meal value price is what will bring in the lower-income crowd.
