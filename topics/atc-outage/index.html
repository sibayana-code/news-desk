Status

Where things stand

The FAA has lifted the nationwide ground stop. Departures were allowed to resume at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on Friday (9:15 a.m. Saturday PH) after more than four hours, though Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy warned that getting flights moving again would be slow and told passengers to check with their airline. FlightRadar24, cited by Reuters after the stop was lifted, counted more than 4,700 cancellations and 10,000 delays on Friday, with about 700 Saturday flights already canceled; aviation analysts told Reuters it could take days to restore schedules. As of about 10:00 a.m. Saturday PH, FlightAware still listed 6,283 cancellations and 8,781 delays within, into or out of the United States for its current day.

Officials describe a temporary telecommunications outage that hit radar and radio communications at Air Route Traffic Control Centers, and later at TRACONs as well. At a White House briefing Friday night, Duffy said that at 3:36 p.m. Eastern the FAA saw instability on an “incredibly old” analog system, that switches and routers became overloaded and began returning inconsistent data, and that the agency shut the system down. Mullin added that the trouble came from “putting patches into the system” as new equipment was installed — “the new technology was overwhelming the older system,” per WIRED. The network involved is reported to be the L3Harris-run FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure (FTI), which L3Harris has not commented on. The precise trigger is still under investigation. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has said there is no indication of a “nefarious attack,” that the problem appears mechanically driven, and that investigators have not completely ruled out a cyber cause.

FAA technicians are investigating. The FBI has been examining whether a software update was involved and, according to a person familiar with the matter, does not believe the outage was a cyberattack or a crime. House and Senate Democrats have called for answers and hearings. Duffy said it will “take us some time to get back to capacity,” that he hopes for “far better movement” on Saturday, and that he expects the system to be fully operational before Thanksgiving travel. Next comes the airline recovery — aircraft, crews and gates are out of position — and a clearer official account of what failed in the network.

    FlightAware cancellations

  2. Sat 14 Nov, about 9:50 AM PH

    Mullin points to patches on new equipment overwhelming the old network; Duffy expects better movement Saturday

    At the same White House briefing, Mullin said the outage stemmed from “putting patches into the system” while new equipment was being installed, and that “the new technology was overwhelming the older system,” WIRED reported. WIRED notes the FTI backbone, run by L3Harris, is midway through a copper-to-fiber modernization. Duffy said it would “take us some time to unwind what has happened,” hoped for “far better movement” on Saturday, and said he expects the system to be fully operational before Thanksgiving.

    WIRED · ABC News live updates

  3. Sat 14 Nov, about 9:41 AM PH

    Duffy says overloaded routers produced inconsistent data

    At a White House briefing, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said that at 3:36 p.m. Eastern the FAA “experienced instability for our radar and our communications in our centers and our TRACONs across the country.” He described an “incredibly old air traffic control system” that uses analog telecom: “Our switches, our routers, actually got overloaded with data … and started to give us inconsistency in the data from those routers. And because of that, we shut the system down.”

    ABC News live updates

  4. Sat 14 Nov, about 9:28 AM PH

    Mullin: it appears mechanically driven; a cyber cause is not fully ruled out

    Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told reporters, “I just want to reiterate this: The public was never at danger through this process.” Pressed on whether officials could be certain it was not a cyberattack, he said, “There’s nothing completely certain right now,” then, “It doesn’t appear that way.” He added that nothing indicated a claim of responsibility and that “it appears that this was mechanically driven. However, we’re still looking, so we’re not going to rule that out.”

    ABC News live updates

  5. Sat 14 Nov, 9:15 AM PH

    FAA lifts the nationwide ground stop

    Duffy said: “At 8:15 tonight, Eastern time, we’ve opened up the airspace. Now does that mean every flight is going to fly right now? It’s going to be slow. So if you’re watching, you’re at an airport, check with your airline.” Air traffic control and airline officials separately told The Air Current the stop was lifted at 8:15 p.m. ET, after more than four hours. Reuters, citing FlightRadar24, later put Friday’s disruption at more than 4,700 cancellations and 10,000 delays, with another 700 Saturday flights already canceled, and said analysts expect days of recovery. It was the longest nationwide shutdown since the September 11, 2001 attacks; the only other nationwide stop since then was about 90 minutes in January 2023, after the FAA’s NOTAM system failed.

    CBS News, via AOL · The Air Current · Reuters

  6. Sat 14 Nov, 8:49 AM PH

    Congressional Democrats demand a full investigation

    Rep. Ted Lieu called the outage “completely unacceptable,” said the FAA needs systems without a single point of failure, and urged congressional hearings. Sen. Tammy Duckworth, the ranking Democrat on the Senate aviation subcommittee, said “the safety of our skies is built on redundancies” and that whatever caused the stop “will warrant a thorough and complete investigation.”

    Los Angeles Times

  7. Sat 14 Nov, 8:15 AM PH

    Some transatlantic flights, and one from Chicago, leave while the stop is still in force

    FlightRadar24 showed some eastbound transatlantic departures from Northeast airports being cleared, and Simple Flying reported the first departure from Chicago O’Hare at about 7:15 p.m. Eastern. The nationwide stop remained in effect for domestic flights at that hour. Alaska Air Group said flights within Alaska, neighbor-island flights in Hawaii, West Coast–Hawaii flights, and some international flights from Honolulu had already been exempted.

    Simple Flying · MyNorthwest

  8. Sat 14 Nov, about 7:00 AM PH

    FAA says the stop remains, with departures as centers recover

    Shortly after 6 p.m. Eastern, the FAA said the nationwide ground stop was still in effect. “As enroute facilities recover, flights will be allowed to depart through coordination with the air traffic control system command center.”

    ABC News live updates

  9. Sat 14 Nov, 6:00 AM PH

    Mullin: no sign of a “nefarious attack,” and this is not another 9/11

    In a roughly 5 p.m. Eastern briefing outside the White House, Mullin said there were no current indications the outages were tied to a cyberattack or a terror threat, calling it “just a telecommunications outage.” He said, “There’s no reason for the public to be afraid or scared for your loved ones. This isn’t another 9/11, I just can’t repeat that enough.” He also said it was “too early to tell” how long the outage or the stop would last, and that as part of the investigation officials were “looking at” any possible link to the FAA’s new SMART traffic-management tool. Aircraft already airborne were allowed to continue to their destinations.

    The Air Current · CBS New York

  10. Sat 14 Nov, 4:53 AM PH

    Command Center issues a nationwide ground stop

    ATCSCC Advisory 111 ordered all U.S. airports ground-stopped “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” from 13/2053Z to 14/0400Z, with inbound international flights to be handled case by case. The FAA’s public statement said the stop followed “communication issues impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers” and that technicians were investigating. The Air Current reported the order went to airlines at 3:44 p.m. Eastern, and that flights already airborne could continue.

    ATCSCC Advisory 111 · FAA statement · The Air Current

  11. Sat 14 Nov, 4:30–4:36 AM PH

    The outage starts in Chicago and spreads to other centers

    The Air Current reported that the FAA command center in Washington was notified around 3:30 p.m. Eastern that multiple centers had lost communications, radio frequencies and radar. The trouble began at the Chicago en-route center, with Boston, Washington, New York and Minneapolis reporting problems in rapid succession. Controllers first moved toward a ground stop for traffic in the eastern system; as more facilities failed, the stop was extended nationwide. CBS News and NBC Chicago reported that, moments earlier, both Chicago O’Hare and Midway had already been placed on ground stops because of an equipment outage. A dispatcher at one airline told The Air Current the command center had said the outage was affecting 10 en-route centers “and growing.”

    The Air Current · CBS News, via AOL · NBC Chicago

Unconfirmed: theories and analysis

Each item is attributed to a named outlet or expert. None of these is an official finding.

A software update on the L3Harris-run FAA telecom network caused a reboot and crash

Multiple sources told CBS News that an update to the private communications network connecting the air-traffic system caused it to reboot, then crash, producing the outage. The system is operated by L3Harris, which had not commented when CBS, Scientific American and The Register asked. The Register notes that the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure, managed by L3Harris, links more than 4,400 facilities. Bloomberg, citing a person familiar with the matter, said the FBI is examining whether a software update caused the outage.

Why it might be true: It fits Duffy’s later account of overloaded routers and switches, and The Air Current’s reporting that the failure began at one center and cascaded. Why it might be false: The FAA has not confirmed a software update or named a contractor. L3Harris has not commented, and the root cause is still under investigation.

CBS News, via AOL · The Register · Bloomberg, via Moneycontrol

The new SMART AI traffic tool, expanded on 10 November, played a role

Mullin said officials were “looking at” any possible relationship to SMART, the FAA’s Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories system, which Duffy said was activated at all 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers on 10 November. Congressional Democrats had already asked for a pause after the first rollout, citing transparency and safety concerns. Air Space Intelligence, which built SMART, said: “The current ground stop is not based on or a result of the SMART system, any product of ASI or our integration with FAA and ATC.”

Why it might be true: The nationwide expansion was only three days old, and the DHS secretary said it was part of the inquiry. Why it might be false: The vendor denies any role, and Duffy’s briefing pointed at analog switches and routers on the telecom network, not a traffic-flow recommendation tool.

The Air Current · Los Angeles Times · The Register

A Voice Switching and Control System update went wrong

Ruth Stilwell, a former air traffic controller and aerospace policy expert, told Scientific American the outage may have been a software problem, “such as a software update gone awry in the Voice Switching and Control System, which manages communications between pilots and air traffic controllers.” She said it would be unusual to push an update during the day, but that the FAA is short-staffed. She also said the problem was not Microsoft software.

Why it might be true: A voice-switch failure would explain lost radio contact between controllers and pilots, which is what centers reported. Why it might be false: Stilwell presented this as speculation. Officials have talked about routers and switches in general and have not named VSCS.

Scientific American

A cyberattack or criminal sabotage

Because this was only the third nationwide ground stop, after September 11, 2001 and the 2023 NOTAM failure, a hostile act was an early question. Mullin said there was no indication of a “nefarious attack,” that the public should not be afraid, and later that the problem appears mechanically driven, while refusing to rule a cyber cause out entirely. A person familiar with the matter told Bloomberg the FBI does not believe the communications failure was a cyberattack or a crime.

Why it might be true: A near-simultaneous failure across many centers is the sort of event investigators would test for an attack, and officials have not closed that door. Why it might be false: DHS, the FBI source and Duffy’s mechanical explanation all currently point the other way, and Mullin said no one had claimed responsibility.

ABC News live updates · Bloomberg, via Moneycontrol

A link to the global Microsoft 365 outage

A major Microsoft 365 disruption, especially to email, ran from Thursday into Friday and prompted questions about a connection. Mullin said the ground stop was not connected to that outage, MyNorthwest reported. The New York Times, cited by MyNorthwest, reported that Microsoft said it had no indication the ground stop was related. The Register likewise found no present indication the two incidents were related. Stilwell told Scientific American the FAA problem was not Microsoft software.

Why it might be true: The two failures overlapped, which made a shared-infrastructure theory reasonable to test. Why it might be false: Both Mullin and Microsoft have now said they see no link, and the FAA’s own description has been of its telecom and radar network, not office software.

MyNorthwest · The Register · Scientific American
