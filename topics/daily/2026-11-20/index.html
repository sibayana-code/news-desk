Gospel of the day

From the Gospel according to Luke 19:45-48

Jesus entered the temple area and proceeded to drive out those who were selling things, saying to them, “It is written, My house shall be a house of prayer, but you have made it a den of thieves.” And every day he was teaching in the temple area. The chief priests, the scribes, and the leaders of the people, meanwhile, were seeking to put him to death, but they could find no way to accomplish their purpose because all the people were hanging on his words.

The words of the Popes

The Temple is an icon of the Church. The Church always — always! — will suffer the temptation of worldliness and the temptation of a power that is not the power that Jesus Christ wants for her! Jesus does not say: “No, this is not done. Get them out.” He says: “You have made a den of thieves here!” And when the Church enters into this process of degradation, the end is very bad. Very bad! There is always in the Church the temptation of corruption. It is when the Church, instead of being attached to fidelity to the Lord Jesus, the Lord of peace, of joy, of salvation, when instead of doing this she is attached to money and power. This happens here, in this Gospel. (…) Jesus drives out of the Temple not the priests, the scribes; he drives out these people who were doing business, the businessmen of the Temple. But the chief priests and the scribes were connected with them: there was the “holy kickback” there! They received from them, they were attached to money and they venerated this saint. The Gospel is very strong. It says: “The chief priests and the scribes were seeking to have Jesus put to death, and so were the leaders of the people. (…) But they did not know what to do because all the people hung on his lips, listening to him.” The strength of Jesus was his word, his witness, his love. And where Jesus is, there is no room for worldliness, there is no room for corruption! (…) Jesus had told us that one cannot serve two masters: either God or riches; either God or power. (Pope Francis, Homily, Casa Santa Marta, 20 November 2015)

Word

zeal /ziːl/ (noun) — great energy or eagerness in service of a person, cause or ideal.

From Middle English zele, via Old French and Late Latin zelus, from Greek zēlos “ardour, jealousy.”

zeal — Luke 19:45-48: Jesus drives the sellers out, “My house shall be a house of prayer.” Pope Francis: where Jesus is, there is no room for worldliness or corruption. Revelation 10: the scroll sweet in the mouth, sour in the stomach. The Way 4: no excuses, build character.
