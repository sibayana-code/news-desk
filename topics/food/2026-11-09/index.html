Eating out is getting dearer faster than groceries as the NCR wage hike and power costs reach the menu

Tape

  • PSA, October · restaurant and accommodation services inflation rose to 7.7% from 7.0%, while food and non-alcoholic drinks eased to 6.1% from 6.7%. PSA's Mapa blames energy, food and raw-material costs plus the NCR wage hike. (BusinessWorld, 5 Nov)
  • NCR minimum wage · up ₱60 since 26 Sep: ₱755 a day for non-agricultural workers, ₱718 for retail, service and small manufacturing. (BusinessWorld, 5 Nov)
  • Rice · local regular milled averaged ₱49.87/kg in October, up 24.17% on the year; well-milled ₱56.19/kg. Mapa says El Niño is now feeding into palay output. (BusinessWorld, 5 Nov)
  • DA · keeping fuel and toll subsidies and lower tariffs on feed corn, and widening the ₱20/kg Benteng Bigas program to seniors, solo parents and PWDs. Corn inflation 21.5%, fruit 10.8%, meat down 3.6% for the poorest 30% of households. (DA, 5 Nov)

Takes

  • When restaurant prices outrun grocery prices, customers notice the gap and cook at home more. Push value you control, like rice refills, bundled sides and a cheaper weekday set, rather than another across-the-board menu hike. Open
  • Meat is the one cheap input right now while rice and corn run hot. Lean on pork and chicken in December specials and keep rice portions measured, not eyeballed. Open

Inflation surges to near 4-year high in October

5 Nov 2026 · BusinessWorld

Headline inflation hit 7.8% in October, the fastest since February 2023, as energy costs spilled into transport, utilities and restaurant services. Dining-out inflation picked up to 7.7% from 7.0%, which PSA chief Dennis Mapa put down to second-round effects of energy, food and raw-material costs and the ₱60 NCR minimum wage hike that took effect on 26 September. Food at home cooled to 6.1% on better weather and supply, but rice kept climbing, with local regular milled up 24% on the year to ₱49.87 a kilo. Mapa warned that El Niño is now feeding through to food prices.

Operator read: this is the squeeze in one line. Your labor, power and rice lines are all rising at once, and your guests can see that a grocery basket is rising more slowly than your menu. Calamba sits outside the NCR wage order, so a Laguna kitchen has a real labor-cost edge over a Makati one right now; that is an argument for commissary or prep work south of Manila. On pricing, raise selectively on items where you lead the market and hold the entry-price items that drive traffic. Rice is the item to watch: at ₱50 a kilo and rising into El Niño, unlimited-rice offers need a hard look before Q1.
