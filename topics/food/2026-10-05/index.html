Fruitas lands ₱750M from PNB — kiosks, bakeries, and brand imports

5 Oct 2026

Philstar and Bilyonaryo: Philippine National Bank has extended a ₱750-million credit facility to Fruitas Holdings Inc. CEO Lester Yu says it gives the group room to accelerate store expansion, strengthen its brand portfolio, and pursue opportunities locally and abroad. Fruitas began nearly 25 years ago as a single fruit-shake kiosk and now runs beverage kiosks, bakeries, community stores, and casual dining nationwide. In 2024 it bought a majority stake in the owner of roasted-chicken brand Mang Bok’s for ₱8.86 million, and its subsidiary Balai ni Fruitas acquired the 40-year-old Sugarhouse brand. Director Calvin Chua says the group is looking at “both inbound and outbound”: bringing foreign brands into the country and taking its own brands overseas. PNB vice chairman Lucio Tan III frames it as giving homegrown companies “financial runway” to compete on a bigger stage. Operator read: this is debt, not equity, so Fruitas has to earn it back store by store. Expect the money to go first into kiosk and bakery density, since those are cheap, fast formats, and that means more competition for the same mall kiosk slots and foot-traffic corners a Calamba opener would want. The inbound line matters more. A listed local operator with fresh credit, shopping for foreign franchise rights, competes with you for the next hot import brand. Two things to steal: the cheap roll-up (Mang Bok’s cost less than ₱9M) and the habit of buying tired heritage brands like Sugarhouse instead of building from zero. If a legacy Laguna bakery or lechon name is quietly for sale, that’s a play that doesn’t need ₱750M.

No US sugar shipments this year — lowest crop in two decades stays home

5 Oct 2026

BusinessMirror: In a hybrid meeting hosted by the Philippine Sugar Millers Association, federations, millers, refiners, and traders agreed to classify all crop year 2026–2027 output as “B,” meaning for domestic use, which skips the US tariff-rate quota for now. The Sugar Regulatory Administration projects raw sugar production at 1.662 million metric tons, the lowest in two decades, hit by red-striped soft scale insect infestation and El Niño dry spells. SRA Administrator Pablo Luis Azcona says USDA had asked the Philippines to fill a 147,000 MT US quota. Some groups had pitched setting aside 4 to 10 percent as “A” export sugar, but most stakeholders said exporting under tight supply would be hard to explain at home. Azcona wants a second meeting in November to reassess prices and validate the output projection. Separately, the House is prioritizing the TUBO Act (House Bill 9088) on industry funding, import policy, price support, and mill accountability. Operator read: no price number in this story, so don’t reprice the menu on it. But a twenty-year-low crop plus a November checkpoint is a clear supply signal. If you run beverages, desserts, bakery, or marinades heavy on sugar, ask suppliers for fixed quotes through the first quarter now, and line up a second supplier before the November meeting, not after. If the November reassessment points to imports, the next fight is over the timing of import orders, and that is when retail sugar moves.

Davao books ₱3.4B “opportunities” in Nanning — durian leads, most still unsigned

17–21 Sep 2026 (reported 1 Oct)

SunStar / DA-Davao: Six Davao agribusinesses at the 23rd China-ASEAN Expo in Nanning reported ₱3.41 billion in initial sales and business opportunities. The breakdown matters: ₱1.54 million in retail sales, ₱32.13 million in booked sales, and ₱3.38 billion still under negotiation. Products on show included fresh and frozen durian, Cavendish bananas, banana chips, processed durian and mango, and coconut charcoal. Raspina Tropical Fruits Inc. signed a contract with a Guangxi importer for 14 MT of fresh durian on Sept. 20. DA-11 spent ₱1.98 million on the trip. The Philippines was the expo’s Country of Honor this year. SunStar itself cautions that the headline is an initial market result, not completed sales. Operator read: the useful part is the product list, not the total. Chinese buyers are asking for processed durian, mango, and banana products, which are shelf-stable value-add lines rather than just fresh fruit. That is the same direction as the saba and mango export deals earlier this quarter. For a Luzon operator this doesn’t move your costs yet. If you want an angle, it is processing: ready-made durian and banana-based products sell to export buyers and to local dessert menus alike. Watch whether the ₱3.38B in talks turns into booked orders by the Q4 DA update.
