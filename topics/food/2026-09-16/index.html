McD leans on Cebu — and power bills — to defend ₱99

8 Sep 2026

Philstar / The Freeman: McDonald’s Philippines is treating Visayas and Mindanao as the next growth engine, not a mature afterthought. Managing director Margot Torres put the country in McD’s global top 10 by store count and top five for growth (behind only China, Japan, and Brazil among fastest-growing, alongside India). Cebu alone: 38 restaurants after three decades; Marigondon and Tipolo (Mandaue) already open this year; five more before year-end — SRP Talisay, Casamira in Guadalupe, Yati in Liloan, Compostela, plus an ICM Cebu relocate. Visayas ~100 and Mindanao ~75 still make up only about one-fifth of the 862-store national network. The cost side is as important as the map: utilities run ~6–7% of sales, electricity ~72% of that utility spend. Seventeen Cebu stores already sit on COREnergy (Vivant’s RES), with a path toward 10–14% power savings in some sites and a broader VisMin RAP build (59 stores / ~2.2 MW contestable load in a Sep 3 Power Philippines follow-on). Torres’s public vow: keep the ₱99 Sulit Busog and other entry tickets even as fuel, wages, and inflation bite — “find other levers in the operation.” COO read: this is how a scaled QSR holds a price war without gutting margin — regional density plus energy procurement, then promo volume. If your Laguna ticket still “absorbs” diesel and Meralco, you are funding the guest. Match the lever order before you match McD’s price point.

Makro returns — Broadfield Biñan and Evo City on the south corridor

21–22 Aug 2026

Rappler / Ayala Land: Makro is back in the Philippines nearly 20 years after SM folded the old SHV/Ayala/SM joint venture into SM Hypermarkets (2009). This time it is an Ayala Corporation–CP Axtra (Thailand) joint venture, with Ayala Land leasing four estate sites: Cloverleaf (Balintawak / EDSA–NLEX), Broadfield in Biñan, Laguna, Arca South (Taguig, inside Ayala Malls Arca South), and Evo City in Kawit, Cavite. Target open: Q4 2026–Q1 2027, with Cloverleaf and Arca South first. Format is classic cash-and-carry — fresh and dry food, imports, non-food — aimed at both households and small businesses, with an omni-channel layer. Broadfield is framed as Ayala Land’s first Laguna business campus (manufacturing, logistics, BPO locators). Evo City’s main mall building is separately slated for Q4 2026, with Makro among the anticipated anchors. Opener/COO take for Calamba: Biñan Broadfield is your nearest large-format wholesale bet on the SLEX/CALAX belt — membership shopping for dry goods and imports without driving into Metro Manila. Watch lease-to-open timing, not the press release; when Makro turns on in Broadfield, reprice your top 20 SKUs against mall wholesalers and current distributors. Evo City + Makro is the Cavite twin — same corridor thesis as Century’s Gen. Trias premium push, but for operator procurement, not just dinner demand.

DA: storms and Habagat lift veggie and fish prices

7 Sep 2026

BusinessWorld / DA: Agriculture Secretary Francisco P. Tiu Laurel Jr. told reporters on the sidelines of the DA budget hearing that high-value crop and fish prices rose after recent tropical cyclones and the enhanced southwest monsoon. Vegetables saw a “significant” jump; fish climbed as fisherfolk stayed ashore. Pork stayed stable. He expects prices to normalize in about three weeks to a month. DA Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Ops Center tally: ₱1.37 billion damage to high-value crops, ₱412.84 million to fisheries. Separately, PSA August inflation eased to 6.1% with food inflation at 4.6%, but rice inflation still ran hot at 19.4% — and Mapa already flagged late-August vegetable firmness from weather. Kitchen take: this is a short, sharp produce and seafood window, not a permanent reset. Dual-source greens, lean menus toward pork and pantry proteins for 3–4 weeks, and don’t let “temporary” become a quiet margin leak on specials. If your supplier is still quoting pre-storm, ask what harvest they are actually delivering.
