Rice imports brought in before region feels worst of El Niño

2 Nov 2026

DA Assistant Secretary Arnel de Mesa said the main reason the government stayed open to rice imports is that Vietnam, the Philippines’ top supplier, is also going to be hit. If the country held off and tried to import next year, he said, exporters might have nothing left to sell. The government is preparing for an El Niño that could last until April or May 2027. At the ASEAN Climate Forum, a draft consensus pointed to a very strong El Niño across Southeast Asia from December to February, with above-normal temperatures in parts of northern Philippines and northern Vietnam and below-normal rain in southern Vietnam’s Mekong Delta, which grows about half of Vietnam’s rice. As of Oct 22 the Philippines had received 2.78 million MT of rice from Vietnam.

Operator read: yesterday’s worry was price, from the weak peso and freight. Today’s story is about supply, and DA is saying so openly. If Vietnam’s dry-season crop disappoints, exporters will hold stock or reprice, and buyers on spot terms get squeezed first. Three moves. Ask your rice supplier how much of their stock is already landed versus still on order. Lock a fixed price for your core variety through February if they’ll give it. And write down your fallback: which blend or grade you’d switch to, and what it does to plate cost, so the decision is already made if your usual sack runs short.

Gyukatsu BGC set to open at Park Triangle Mall in Nov

6 Oct 2026 · Promo (press release, Manila Times newswire)

Gyukatsu Matsumura, a Japanese beef-cutlet restaurant that built its following in Boracay, is opening its second branch at Park Triangle Mall in northern BGC this November. Diners finish each slice of panko-crusted beef on a personal hot stone at the table. Founder Kevin Tan said Manila guests who found the restaurant on vacation kept asking when it would come to the city.

Operator read: this is a paid release, so take the claims lightly, but the format bet is worth noting. Tabletop finishing pushes the cooking to the guest, which keeps kitchen labor and skill needs low while the premium beef carries the price. It lands as beef inflation runs in the high double digits for big chains, so watch its menu pricing when it opens. It shows how much a premium beef concept can charge in BGC right now.
