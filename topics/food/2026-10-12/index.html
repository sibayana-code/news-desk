Jollibee pools 189 stores to buy power from First Gen, and says more stores are coming

11 Oct 2026

BusinessWorld: Jollibee Group widened its renewable-energy partnership with Lopez-led First Gen to cover 189 company-owned stores in the Philippines under the government’s retail aggregation program, which lets eligible sites combine their demand and buy electricity as a group. The stores make up about 40% of JFC’s company-owned PH outlets and cover six brands: Jollibee, Greenwich, Chowking, Red Ribbon, Burger King and Panda Express. “By consolidating eligible stores and improving electricity sourcing, we are demonstrating a practical approach that supports operational efficiency,” said Bong Filart, head of network development at Jollibee Group-Philippines. He added that JFC plans to expand the program to more eligible stores nationwide. The deal builds on earlier work with First Gen: more than 9 MW of solar panels (17,000 panels) at JFC’s Parañaque and Laguna commissaries since 2023, and 1 MW of renewable supply this year for its biggest Visayas-Mindanao plant in Cebu. Operator read: the biggest chain in the country is treating power as a cost to buy down, not a bill to pay. JFC can do it because it has hundreds of stores to pool. A mid-size group can’t match that, but the route is open. Get a year of bills per store, find out from your mall or a retail electricity supplier whether your sites qualify, alone or grouped, and price a contract against your current rate. Then put the commissary first: that is where JFC started with solar, and it is where a Laguna operator’s load is steadiest.

DA: rice is covered by imports into 2027, but vegetables are a gamble with December typhoons

12 Oct 2026

Manila Times: Agriculture Secretary Francisco Tiu Laurel Jr. said the department is bracing for El Niño effects that run well into next year. DA won’t block rice imports. Arrivals reached 3.67 million tonnes by end-August, against 2.95 million a year earlier, and Tiu Laurel had told Congress that full-year imports could hit 5 million tonnes. DA says imports and seasonal harvests should keep rice prices fairly stable through early 2027. Vegetables are another matter. “When it comes to vegetable supplies it is unpredictable,” he said, adding that “there’s a high chance of strong typhoons in December.” DA’s damage count from four recent storms (Luis, Maymay, Neneng and Pilandok) and the enhanced southwest monsoon puts rice losses at ₱2.05 billion across 73,409 hectares, or 65,106 tonnes. That is about 0.32% of the year’s 20.29-million-tonne target. Operator read: split your cost planning in two. Rice is the one staple where government is openly managing supply, so don’t pay up for long rice contracts out of fear. Vegetables are where December can hurt, right when you need volume for parties and catering. Lock vegetable prices for December with a supplier who can show you a non-Benguet backup, pre-cost a vegetable-light version of your best sellers, and keep holiday set menus flexible on the vegetable side so one storm doesn’t wipe out the margin.
