ALDI SÜD buys into DALI — hard discount gets German scale capital

7–8 Sep 2026

Insider PH / Inside Retail Asia: ALDI SÜD Group took an undisclosed minority stake in Philippine hard-discount grocer DALI, keeping the chain under local management while funding more stores, jobs, and supplier partnerships. DALI (est. 2020) runs a European-style hard-discount model — tight assortments, private-label FMCG, low-overhead neighborhood boxes — past 1,300 locations and more than a million customers a day. It claims nearly 10,000 jobs and ties to hundreds of Philippine farmers and manufacturers. ALDI SOUTH CFO Marcus Almeling framed the Philippines as high-growth demand for quality groceries at low prices and called the bet long-term. Stake size and cash not disclosed. COO/opener read: this is not another Ayala or SM mall story. It is barangay grocery density with German discount DNA — private-label staples that reset what “cheap and near” means next to your restaurant. Map DALI openings against your Laguna and Cavite catchment; when the discounter thickens a barangay, merienda and grocery-substitution behavior moves before mall FOHO rents do.

McD’s Chicken McDo playbook: Gold Standards, local chicken, then the combo

1 Sep 2026

Insider PH: McDonald’s Philippines is publishing the ops layer behind Chicken McDo — detailed “Gold Standards” on appearance, texture, flavor, freshness, and food safety, enforced by about 3,200 trained Chicken Experts across the network. QA director Alvin Marcelo: food safety first, “good enough” is not served; Experts own thaw checks, inventory, cook-to-demand so product stays hot. Chicken McDo and the rice beside it are locally sourced; the company sells millions of pieces a year and audits farms through slaughter. Menu/taste marketing director Celine Lagandaon ties specs to Luzon–Visayas–Mindanao consumer research (size, crisp, juiciness). On the ticket side, a Chicken McDo meal with fillet, rice, and drink sits at ₱158; Mega Meals pair two proteins with a sundae, with Taro Custard Pie from Sep 14. [FLAG: promo.] Operator take: this is the SaladStop-style sourcing story for QSR — local protein + process discipline, then a protein-forward combo. If you are still fighting Jollibee’s ₱99 fillet window on sticker alone, you are missing McD’s real lever order.

DA final: ₱4.38B agri hit — lowland veg +15–20%, Southern Tagalog named

9 Sep 2026

GMA / DA: Final Habagat + cyclones Luis, Maymay, Neneng, and Pilandok assessment puts agricultural damage and losses at ₱4.38 billion and 102,791 metric tons across Cordillera, Ilocos, Cagayan Valley, Central Luzon, CALABARZON, MIMAROPA, Bicol, and Western Visayas. Rice took the biggest peso hit (₱2.05B) but DA says rice retail should hold; high-value crops ₱1.38B; fisheries earlier window still soft. Spokesperson Arnel de Mesa: vegetable prices can rise 15–20%, mostly lowland produce, because Central Luzon and Southern Tagalog were hit; Cordillera upland also firmer but recovers faster. Livestock damage light (₱32.65M). Kitchen take for Calamba: you sit inside the named Southern Tagalog corridor. This is still the same 3–4 week storm window from earlier this month — now with a hard damage total and an explicit percentage band. Dual-source greens, lean specials off storm-priced produce, and do not let a temporary quote become your permanent plate cost.
