Robinsons plants a flag in Panglao tourism

9 Sep 2026

Robinsons Retail Holdings opened its first Robinsons Supermarket in Panglao on Sep 4 at Bellemar Lifestyle Center in Tawala — a bet that Bohol’s tourism hub spends like a city: households plus hotels, resorts, and restaurants that need steady fresh and dry. Group GM Kerwin Legarde framed it as Everyday Goodness / Everyday Value closer to more communities; the real tell is HORECA adjacency. Store hours 8am–9pm. Opener/COO read: tourism corridors don’t wait for Metro density math. When a lifestyle/resort node densifies (Tagaytay rim, Batangas coast, Laguna eco-estates), a full-line grocer will chase the same kitchens you buy from — lock preferred-supplier terms early or watch your landed cost climb when the box arrives.

DA: El Niño prep, high-grade rice curb — storms at P4.38B

9 Sep 2026

DA’s final bulletin on Habagat plus typhoons Luis, Maymay, Neneng, and Pilandok: P4.38B agri damage, 99,663 farmers/fishers, 81,511 ha, 102,791 MT lost across eight regions including CALABARZON. Rice took P2.05B / 65,106 MT — about 0.32% of the 20.29M MT annual target. Aid pool: P609.79M seeds/inputs; NFA 9,260 bags to hit LGUs; PCIC P187M indemnities. Separately Wednesday, DA spokesperson Arnel de Mesa said P6.2B fertilizer and fuel under UPLIFT will tilt to areas less hit by El Niño to max yield; full crop/price impact flagged for 1H 2027; DA wants another P10B in the 2027 budget. De Mesa: storm rice losses still inside yearly weather expectations for price. Policy swing: high-grade rice imports on pause — Secretary Tiu Laurel talked industry into importing only 25% (and above) broken for now. Kitchen read: don’t rewrite the rice menu on today’s soft storm math; watch grade mix and forward contracts before El Niño deepens late 2026.

SEC stops Takoyadon owner’s “co-ownership” raise

9 Sep 2026

SEC issued a cease-and-desist against Nego Food Solution Corp. — operator of the Takoyadon Japanese chain — plus Noel B. Andres, Marko/Mark Marquez, and Celyn/Jocelyn Manzanero for illegally soliciting public investments. Pitch: one-time non-refundable P99,000 “co-ownership” funneled toward SMNV Project Food Corp., then used in Nego Food resto ops; monthly 3% management and 4% system/marketing fees on gross sales; profit (and loss) share with zero investor say — Nego kept full control. Commission’s Howey-style checklist: money in, common enterprise, profit expectation, returns from promoter effort; plus social-media public solicitation of unregistered securities. Warning extends to agents and promoters. Entrepreneur read: Filipino resto expansion capital is getting policed. If a brand sells you a unit stake that looks like a franchise fee but acts like a security, treat the CDO as the market’s new baseline — documented franchise or walk.
