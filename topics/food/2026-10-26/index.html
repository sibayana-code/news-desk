Pork import permits to stop mid-November to January as DA backs a return to 30–40% tariffs

25–26 Oct 2026

BusinessWorld reports the Department of Agriculture will suspend sanitary and phytosanitary import clearances for pork from mid-November through January, covering the holiday peak. Secretary Tiu Laurel also backed restoring tariffs to 30% for in-quota and 40% for out-of-quota shipments, up from today’s 15% and 25% set during the ASF crisis, and has started the groundwork for safeguard measures. GMA News adds that the suspension covers select cuts, applies “once we are legally allowed,” and that DA will consider exemptions for meat processors. SINAG wants a 120-day suspension; DA is holding at two months. The Pork Producers Federation says cheap frozen imports cost the sector ₱350 million a day. The tariff itself cannot move without the Tariff Commission.

Operator read: yesterday this was a proposal; today it has dates. The clearance stop is something DA can do on its own, so treat it as likely, and treat the tariff as a 2027 risk. Imported frozen cuts are what most value menus run on, and importers will price December and January stock to the freeze. Three moves for this week: get written December–January prices from your importer, ask your processed-meat supplier whether they qualify for an exemption, and open a local hog contract while farmgate is still below cost. If you can shift one or two pork SKUs to chicken for Q1, start testing now rather than in January.
